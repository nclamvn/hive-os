## Proof (independent critical verification)

**Reduction modulo \(2\).** For every real \(\alpha\), choosing \(q=\lfloor\alpha/2\rfloor\) gives \(r=\alpha-2q\in[0,2)\), including when \(\alpha<0\). The implicit identity
\[
\lfloor m+x\rfloor=m+\lfloor x\rfloor\qquad(m\in\mathbb Z)
\]
holds because translating the inequalities defining \(\lfloor x\rfloor\) by an integer preserves them. Applying it with \(m=2qk\) proves
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lfloor k\alpha\rfloor
=q\,n(n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lfloor kr\rfloor.
\]
The first term is an integer multiple of \(n\), so the claimed equivalence is valid in both directions.

**Case \(0<r<1\).** For \(n=\lceil1/r\rceil\), the ceiling inequalities give
\[
n-1<1/r\le n<1/r+1.
\]
Thus \(n\ge2\), every \(k<n\) satisfies \(0<kr<1\), and
\[
1\le nr<1+r<2.
\]
The sum is therefore exactly \(1\), which \(n\ge2\) cannot divide. If \(1/r\) is an integer, then \(nr=1\); its floor is still \(1\), so equality causes no exception.

**Case \(1\le r<2\).** Here \(\beta=2-r\in(0,1]\). Setting \(n=\lfloor1/\beta\rfloor+1\) gives
\[
n-1\le1/\beta<n,\qquad n\le1/\beta+1.
\]
Consequently \(n\ge2\), and for every \(k<n\),
\[
0<k\beta\le1.
\]
Hence \(2k-1\le kr<2k\), proving \(\lfloor kr\rfloor=2k-1\). At the last index,
\[
1<n\beta\le1+\beta\le2,
\]
so \(2n-2\le nr<2n-1\), proving \(\lfloor nr\rfloor=2n-2\). The sum is exactly
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2k-1)-1=n^2-1\equiv-1\pmod n,
\]
and is not divisible by \(n\).

The endpoint \(r=1\) is covered: \(\beta=1\), \(n=2\), and the sum is \(3\). When \(1/\beta\) is an integer, an earlier term may satisfy \(k\beta=1\); the inclusive lower bound still gives the asserted floor. The possible equality \(n\beta=2\) likewise gives the asserted last floor. The excluded endpoint \(r=2\) cannot arise from the reduction.

**Necessity and sufficiency.** These two cases exclude every \(r>0\) in the reduced interval, leaving only \(r=0\). Conversely, for any \(q\in\mathbb Z\), positive, zero, or negative, each \(2qk\) is an integer, so
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lfloor2qk\rfloor=q\,n(n+1)
\]
is divisible by \(n\). The case \(n=1\) is automatic; all contradiction witnesses correctly have \(n\ge2\).

## Scope and gaps

This audit supports the entire supplied proof. There is no circular reasoning, missing boundary case, essential gap, or reliance on finite computation. The floor-translation identity could be stated explicitly for readability, but its omission is not a mathematical defect.
