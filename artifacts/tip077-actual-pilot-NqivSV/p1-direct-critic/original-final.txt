**Answer.** The supplied attempt is correct. Its conclusion is exactly \(\alpha\in2\mathbb Z\), and I find no substantive objection.

**Proof (audit of the supplied argument).**

1. **The average-error formula is valid for every real \(\alpha\).** Divisibility gives \(q_n=S_n/n\in\mathbb Z\). Using
   \[
   \lfloor k\alpha\rfloor=k\alpha-\{k\alpha\}
   \]
   and \(\sum_{k=1}^n k=n(n+1)/2\) gives
   \[
   q_n=\frac{\alpha(n+1)}2-e_n.
   \]
   Each fractional part lies in \([0,1)\), so its finite average \(e_n\) also lies in \([0,1)\). This remains true for negative \(\alpha\).

2. **The formula for \(d_n\) has the correct indexing and algebra.** For \(n\ge2\),
   \[
   nq_n-(n-1)q_{n-1}=\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor.
   \]
   Since \(q_{n-1}=\alpha n/2-e_{n-1}\), it follows that
   \[
   d_n=\frac{\alpha}{2}
       +\frac{e_{n-1}-\{n\alpha\}}n.
   \]
   The numerator of the error belongs to \((-1,1)\), establishing the strict bound
   \[
   \left|d_n-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right|<\frac1n.
   \]
   Also, \(d_n\) is an integer because both \(q_n\) and \(q_{n-1}\) are integers.

3. **The constancy and limiting steps are rigorous.** For any \(n,m\ge3\),
   \[
   |d_n-d_m|<\frac1n+\frac1m\le\frac23<1.
   \]
   An integer of absolute value less than \(1\) is zero, so every \(d_n\), \(n\ge3\), equals \(d=d_3\). If
   \(\delta=|d-\alpha/2|>0\), the Archimedean property supplies an integer
   \(n>\max(3,1/\delta)\), contradicting \(\delta<1/n\).
   Thus \(\alpha=2d\). This is an argument over arbitrarily large \(n\), not a finite-computation inference.

4. **Sufficiency includes negative even integers.** If \(\alpha=2d\) for any \(d\in\mathbb Z\), every \(k\alpha\) is an integer, hence
   \[
   S_n=2d\sum_{k=1}^n k=d\,n(n+1),
   \]
   which is divisible by \(n\), regardless of the sign of \(d\).

**Scope and gaps.** No essential gap or correction is needed. The case \(n=1\) is automatic; \(d_n\) is defined only for \(n\ge2\), so no undefined \(q_0\) occurs. The argument covers zero, negative numbers, rational numbers, and irrational numbers without additional assumptions. This is a verification of the original attempt.
