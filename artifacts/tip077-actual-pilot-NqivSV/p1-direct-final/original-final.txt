**Answer.** Exactly the even integers:
\[
\boxed{\alpha\in 2\mathbb Z}.
\]

**Proof.** Suppose the divisibility condition holds. For every positive integer \(n\), define
\[
S_n=\sum_{k=1}^n\lfloor k\alpha\rfloor,
\qquad q_n=\frac{S_n}{n}\in\mathbb Z.
\]
For every real \(x\), including negative \(x\), its fractional part
\(\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor\) satisfies \(0\le\{x\}<1\). Thus
\[
q_n=\frac{\alpha(n+1)}2-e_n,
\qquad
e_n=\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n\{k\alpha\}\in[0,1).
\]

For \(n\ge2\), the difference \(d_n=q_n-q_{n-1}\) is an integer. Moreover,
\[
\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor
=S_n-S_{n-1}
=nq_n-(n-1)q_{n-1}
=nd_n+q_{n-1}.
\]
Substituting \(\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor=n\alpha-\{n\alpha\}\) and
\(q_{n-1}=\alpha n/2-e_{n-1}\) gives
\[
d_n=\frac{\alpha}{2}
+\frac{e_{n-1}-\{n\alpha\}}n.
\]
Because both \(e_{n-1}\) and \(\{n\alpha\}\) lie in \([0,1)\),
\[
\left|d_n-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right|<\frac1n.
\]

For any \(n,m\ge3\), it follows that
\[
|d_n-d_m|
<\frac1n+\frac1m
\le\frac23<1.
\]
Since \(d_n-d_m\) is an integer, it must be zero. Hence there is one integer \(d\) such that \(d_n=d\) for every \(n\ge3\), and consequently
\[
\left|d-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right|<\frac1n
\qquad(n\ge3).
\]
If the left-hand side were some \(\delta>0\), choosing an integer
\(n>\max\{3,1/\delta\}\) would contradict this inequality. Therefore
\(\alpha/2=d\), proving \(\alpha\in2\mathbb Z\).

Conversely, let \(\alpha=2d\) with \(d\in\mathbb Z\). Each \(k\alpha\) is an integer, so for every positive integer \(n\),
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\lfloor k\alpha\rfloor
=2d\sum_{k=1}^n k
=d\,n(n+1),
\]
which is divisible by \(n\). This calculation applies equally to negative \(d\) and to \(d=0\).

**Scope and gaps.** Both directions hold for all real \(\alpha\), including negative values. There are no remaining gaps.
