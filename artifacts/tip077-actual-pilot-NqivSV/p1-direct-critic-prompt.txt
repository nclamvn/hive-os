**Answer.** Exactly the even integers:
\[
\boxed{\alpha\in 2\mathbb Z}.
\]

**Proof.** Suppose the stated divisibility holds. Define
\[
S_n=\sum_{k=1}^n\lfloor k\alpha\rfloor,
\qquad q_n=\frac{S_n}{n}\in\mathbb Z.
\]
For any real \(x\), its fractional part \(\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor\) satisfies \(0\le\{x\}<1\), including when \(x\) is negative. Consequently,
\[
q_n=\frac{\alpha(n+1)}2-e_n,
\qquad
e_n=\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n\{k\alpha\}\in[0,1).
\]

For \(n\ge2\), set \(d_n=q_n-q_{n-1}\), an integer. Since
\[
\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor
=S_n-S_{n-1}
=nq_n-(n-1)q_{n-1}
=nd_n+q_{n-1},
\]
we obtain
\[
d_n
=\frac{\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor-q_{n-1}}n
=\frac{\alpha}{2}
+\frac{e_{n-1}-\{n\alpha\}}n.
\]
Both error terms belong to \([0,1)\), so
\[
\left|d_n-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right|<\frac1n.
\]

For any \(n,m\ge3\), this implies
\[
|d_n-d_m|<\frac1n+\frac1m\le\frac23<1.
\]
Two distinct integers differ by at least \(1\); hence all \(d_n\) for \(n\ge3\) equal one integer \(d\). Thus
\[
\left|d-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right|<\frac1n
\quad\text{for every }n\ge3.
\]
This forces \(\alpha/2=d\): any positive difference would exceed \(1/n\) for a sufficiently large integer \(n\). Therefore \(\alpha\) is an even integer.

Conversely, if \(\alpha=2d\) with \(d\in\mathbb Z\), then
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\lfloor k\alpha\rfloor
=\sum_{k=1}^n2dk
=d\,n(n+1),
\]
which is divisible by \(n\) for every positive integer \(n\).

**Scope and gaps.** The proof establishes both directions for all real \(\alpha\), including negative values. There are no unresolved gaps.
