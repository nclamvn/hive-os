## Proof

The essential implications check out as follows.

1. **The bound on multiplicities outside the initial range is correct.**

   Let \(t\) be the first index at which any value greater than \(K\) reaches multiplicity \(K+1\). Each occurrence \(t_i\) of the chosen \(x>K\) lies after \(N\), so its predecessor \(y_i\) satisfies
   \[
   C_{t_i-1}(y_i)=x>K.
   \]
   If \(y_i>K\), that value would already have reached multiplicity \(K+1\) before \(t\), contradicting minimality.

   The predecessors are distinct: at two distinct occurrences of the same predecessor value, its cumulative counts are strictly different. Thus it cannot generate \(x\) twice. The resulting \(K+1\) distinct predecessors in \(\{1,\ldots,K\}\) are impossible.

2. **The construction and identification of \(S\) are justified.**

   Boundedness would give a finite range containing an infinitely recurring value. Its unbounded occurrence counts would then appear as subsequent terms, contradicting boundedness. Hence infinitely many distinct values first appear after \(N\); each first appearance is followed by \(1\). Consequently \(1\in S\).

   The chosen \(L\) bounds every value outside \(S\): finitely recurring values at most \(K\) are covered by its definition, and values greater than \(K\) have multiplicity at most \(K\).

   For \(x>L\), no initial term equals \(x\), and no predecessor outside \(S\) can generate it. Every \(s\in S\) generates \(x\) exactly once, at its \(x\)-th occurrence. **The condition \(L\ge N\) handles the initial-segment boundary:** this occurrence has index at least \(x>N\), so the recurrence applies to its successor.

   Therefore \(x\) occurs exactly \(r=|S|\) times, and those occurrences are followed by \(1,\ldots,r\). Infinitely many different \(x\) give infinitely many occurrences of each of these small values. Thus
   \[
   \{1,\ldots,r\}\subseteq S,
   \]
   and equality follows from cardinality.

3. **The alternating tail follows without an additional assumption.**

   Choose a time after which every value in \(S\) has cumulative count greater than \(L\). A sufficiently late occurrence in \(S\) is followed by a value greater than \(L\). Every such large value has total multiplicity \(r\), so each occurrence is followed by a value in \(\{1,\ldots,r\}=S\).

   Iterating these two implications produces the asserted alternating tail. This also excludes any other values from that tail.

4. **The counter transition has the correct indexing.**

   After the occurrence \(b_m\), the counts of the small values are \(c_i(m+1)\), because the intervening large term contributes to none of these counts. Set
   \[
   u=c_{b_m}(m+1)>L.
   \]
   For each small value \(i\), its unique generation of \(u\) has occurred by the current large term exactly when its \(u\)-th occurrence has happened, equivalently when \(c_i(m+1)\ge u\). Hence the current multiplicity of \(u\), and therefore its successor, is
   \[
   b_{m+1}=\#\{i:c_i(m+1)\ge u\}.
   \]
   Every small value occurs infinitely often in this tail, so every counter tends to infinity.

5. **The invariant is valid, including at ties.**

   Suppose the selected counter has value \(t\) before its increment. Because the counters are integers,
   \[
   q=1+\#\{i:c_i>t\}.
   \]
   Thus \(d_q=t\), and replacing the selected \(t\) by \(t+1\) changes the sorted vector precisely by increasing coordinate \(q\). This remains true when several counters equal \(t\).

   Consequently
   \[
   c_i'=c_i+\mathbf1_{i=b},\qquad
   d_i'=d_i+\mathbf1_{i=q},\qquad b'=q,
   \]
   which proves \(e_i'=e_i\). The resulting bound \(|c_i-d_i|\le E\) is therefore sound.

6. **The large-gap argument correctly yields bounded spread.**

   If \(d_k-d_{k+1}>2E\), the invariant bound implies that every counter labelled \(1,\ldots,k\) exceeds every remaining counter.

   If the selected label belonged to that upper group, its increment would preserve the separation, and its next rank—and therefore next selected label—would again belong to the same group. Inductively, all lower counters would remain constant forever. This contradicts their divergence to infinity.

   Thus the selected counter lies in the lower group. The upper counters remain unchanged, and the maximum lower counter cannot decrease, so this sorted gap cannot increase. An increment cannot move a lower counter above an upper one: their values differ by a positive integer before the increment.

   When the gap is at most \(2E\), a single update increases it by at most \(1\). The stated bound \(B\) therefore follows by induction.

7. **The finite-state conclusion and parity transfer are correct.**

   Bounded spread leaves finitely many normalized counter vectors and selected labels. The update is deterministic and invariant under adding a common constant to all counters. A repeated normalized state therefore gives a periodic future selected-index sequence.

   The small terms occupy every other position of the original tail, with fixed parity. Their eventual periodicity consequently gives eventual periodicity of the corresponding parity subsequence.
