SAME-ARM ORIGINAL ATTEMPT:

**Answer.** The only pair is \(\boxed{(a,b)=(1,1)}\).

**Proof.** Suppose positive integers \(g,N\) satisfy
\[
\gcd(a^n+b,b^n+a)=g\qquad\text{for every }n\ge N.
\]
Set \(m=ab+1\). Then
\[
\gcd(a,m)=\gcd(b,m)=1,
\]
because any common divisor divides both \(ab\) and \(ab+1\).

There exists a positive integer \(t\) such that
\[
a^t\equiv b^t\equiv1\pmod m.
\]
Indeed, among the \(m^2+1\) pairs
\[
(a^r\bmod m,\ b^r\bmod m),\qquad 0\le r\le m^2,
\]
two coincide, at indices \(i<j\). Since \(a\) and \(b\) are invertible modulo \(m\) by Bézout’s identity, cancellation gives the assertion with \(t=j-i\).

Choose a positive integer \(k\) with \(kt-1\ge N\). Since \(ab\equiv-1\pmod m\),
\[
a\bigl(a^{kt-1}+b\bigr)
=a^{kt}+ab\equiv0\pmod m.
\]
Cancelling \(a\) shows \(m\mid a^{kt-1}+b\). Likewise,
\[
b\bigl(b^{kt-1}+a\bigr)\equiv0\pmod m
\]
shows \(m\mid b^{kt-1}+a\). Consequently,
\[
m\mid\gcd(a^{kt-1}+b,b^{kt-1}+a)=g.
\]

At exponent \(kt\), the assumed equality implies that \(g\) divides both \(a^{kt}+b\) and \(b^{kt}+a\). Hence \(m\) divides both, and reducing modulo \(m\) yields
\[
m\mid b+1,\qquad m\mid a+1.
\]
These are positive integers, so
\[
ab+1\le b+1,\qquad ab+1\le a+1.
\]
Thus \(ab\le b\) and \(ab\le a\). Positivity gives \(a\le1\) and \(b\le1\), proving \(a=b=1\).

Conversely, for \(a=b=1\),
\[
\gcd(1^n+1,1^n+1)=2
\]
for every positive integer \(n\). Thus \(g=2,N=1\) suffice.

**Scope and gaps.** This proves necessity and sufficiency for all positive integer pairs. There are no unresolved gaps or computational assumptions.
