## Proof

Define polynomials by
\[
D_0(x)=2,\qquad D_1(x)=x,\qquad
D_n(x)=xD_{n-1}(x)-D_{n-2}(x)\quad(n\ge2).
\]
Choose \(p=D_{19}\). Expanding this recurrence gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(x)={}&x^{19}-19x^{17}+152x^{15}-665x^{13}
 +1729x^{11}-2717x^9\\
&+2508x^7-1254x^5+285x^3-19x.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(p\) has integer coefficients, is odd and monic, has degree \(19\), and has linear coefficient \(-19\).

The recurrence construction is useful because it admits an algebraic substitution yielding explicit factors. A nontrivial polynomial composition cannot provide a construction of degree \(19\), since degrees multiply and \(19\) is prime.

**Factorization of the equality curve.** For an indeterminate \(z\), induction on the recurrence proves
\[
D_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}.
\]
Indeed, this holds for \(n=0,1\), and multiplication by \(z+z^{-1}\), followed by subtraction of the preceding expression, proves the next case.

Let \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/19}\), and put
\[
x=z+z^{-1},\qquad
y_k=\zeta^kz+\zeta^{-k}z^{-1}\quad(0\le k\le18).
\]
The substitution identity gives
\[
p(y_k)=z^{19}+z^{-19}=p(x).
\]
These nineteen \(y_k\) are distinct elements of \(\mathbb C(z)\): equality of two would imply equality of their coefficients of \(z\), hence equality of their indices modulo \(19\). Consequently, since \(p(y)-p(x)\) is monic of degree \(19\) in \(y\),
\[
p(y)-p(x)=\prod_{k=0}^{18}(y-y_k)
\]
after substituting \(x=z+z^{-1}\).

For \(1\le k\le9\), define
\[
a_k=\zeta^k+\zeta^{-k},\qquad
b_k=a_k^2-4=(\zeta^k-\zeta^{-k})^2.
\]
The paired roots satisfy
\[
y_k+y_{19-k}=a_kx,\qquad
y_ky_{19-k}=x^2+b_k.
\]
Also \(y_0=x\). Hence
\[
\boxed{\;
p(y)-p(x)
=(y-x)\prod_{k=1}^{9}
\bigl(y^2-a_kxy+x^2+b_k\bigr).
\;}
\]
This is an identity in \(\mathbb C[x,y]\). To justify passage back from the substitution, the map
\[
\mathbb C[x,y]\longrightarrow\mathbb C[z,z^{-1},y],
\qquad x\longmapsto z+z^{-1},
\]
is injective: for a nonzero polynomial in \(x\) of degree \(d\), its substituted Laurent polynomial has a nonzero coefficient of \(z^d\). Applying this observation to each coefficient in \(y\) proves injectivity.

**Irreducibility and distinctness.** Write
\[
Q_k(x,y)=y^2-a_kxy+x^2+b_k.
\]
Because \(\zeta^k\ne\zeta^{-k}\), we have \(b_k\ne0\). The discriminant of this quadratic in \(y\) is
\[
\Delta_k(x)=a_k^2x^2-4(x^2+b_k)
=b_k(x^2-4).
\]
It is not a square in \(\mathbb C(x)\): its zero at \(x=2\) has order one, whereas every zero or pole of a square of a rational function has even order. A quadratic over a field of characteristic zero is reducible exactly when its discriminant is a square. Therefore \(Q_k\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb C(x)\).

It is also irreducible in \(\mathbb C[x,y]\). Any factorization with both factors of positive degree in \(y\) would contradict field irreducibility; a factor independent of \(y\) would have to divide the leading coefficient \(1\), so would be constant.

The \(Q_k\) are distinct. If \(a_k=a_\ell\), then \(\zeta^\ell\) is one of the two roots \(\zeta^k,\zeta^{-k}\) of
\[
t^2-a_kt+1=0.
\]
For \(1\le k,\ell\le9\), this forces \(k=\ell\). None of these quadratic factors equals the linear factor \(y-x\).

**Projective closure.** In coordinates
\[
x=X_0/X_1,\qquad y=Y_0/Y_1,
\]
the equality curve is defined by
\[
p_h(X_0,X_1)Y_1^{19}
-p_h(Y_0,Y_1)X_1^{19}=0,
\]
where \(p_h\) is the degree-\(19\) homogenization of \(p\).

Bihomogenizing the factorization above yields the diagonal and the closures of the nine irreducible quadratic curves. Each closure remains irreducible, since the closure of an irreducible affine variety is irreducible. There is no component contained in the boundary: setting \(X_1=0\) forces \(Y_1=0\), and conversely, so the boundary intersection consists only of \((\infty,\infty)\).

Thus \(X\) has ten distinct irreducible components. Each quadratic closure has bidegree \((2,2)\) and an irreducible quadratic affine equation, so these components are not linear. In particular, the required component condition holds.

**Evaluation.** Set \(v_n=D_n(19)\). Then
\[
v_0=2,\qquad v_1=19,\qquad
v_n=19v_{n-1}-v_{n-2}.
\]
Successive integer arithmetic gives
\[
v_{17}=5227260815326346060059,\qquad
v_{18}=99042070146811639444802.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(19)=v_{19}
&=19(99042070146811639444802)
  -5227260815326346060059\\
&=\boxed{1876572071974094803391179}.
\end{aligned}
\]
