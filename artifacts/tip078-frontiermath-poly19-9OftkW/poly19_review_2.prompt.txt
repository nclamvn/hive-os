## Proof

Define polynomials recursively by
\[
D_0(t)=2,\qquad D_1(t)=t,\qquad
D_n(t)=tD_{n-1}(t)-D_{n-2}(t)\quad(n\ge2).
\]
Choose \(p=D_{19}\). Expanding this recurrence gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(t)={}&t^{19}-19t^{17}+152t^{15}-665t^{13}
+1729t^{11}-2717t^9\\
&+2508t^7-1254t^5+285t^3-19t.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(p\) has integer, hence real, coefficients. It is odd and monic, has degree \(19\), and its linear coefficient is \(-19\).

We will factor \(p(y)-p(x)\), prove its factors irreducible, and check their projective closures.

For an indeterminate \(z\), the recurrence implies
\[
D_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}.
\]
Indeed, this holds for \(n=0,1\), and the induction step follows from
\[
(z+z^{-1})(z^{n-1}+z^{-(n-1)})
-(z^{n-2}+z^{-(n-2)})=z^n+z^{-n}.
\]

Let \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/19}\). In \(\mathbb C(z)\), put
\[
x=z+z^{-1},\qquad
y_k=\zeta^kz+\zeta^{-k}z^{-1}\quad(0\le k\le18).
\]
Since \((\zeta^kz)^{19}=z^{19}\), the substitution identity gives
\[
p(y_k)=z^{19}+z^{-19}=p(x).
\]
The nineteen \(y_k\) are distinct: equality of two Laurent polynomials would force equality of their coefficients of \(z\), hence equality of their indices modulo \(19\). Because \(p(y)-p(x)\) is monic of degree \(19\) in \(y\), these are all its roots after substitution.

For \(1\le k\le9\), define
\[
a_k=\zeta^k+\zeta^{-k},\qquad
b_k=a_k^2-4=(\zeta^k-\zeta^{-k})^2.
\]
Pairing indices \(k\) and \(19-k\) gives
\[
y_k+y_{19-k}=a_kx
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
y_ky_{19-k}
&=z^2+z^{-2}+\zeta^{2k}+\zeta^{-2k}\\
&=x^2+a_k^2-4=x^2+b_k.
\end{aligned}
\]
Also \(y_0=x\). Therefore
\[
p(y)-p(x)
=(y-x)\prod_{k=1}^{9}Q_k(x,y),
\qquad
Q_k(x,y)=y^2-a_kxy+x^2+b_k.
\tag{1}
\]
This is an identity in \(\mathbb C[x,y]\), not merely an identity after substitution. To see this, the substitution
\[
\mathbb C[x,y]\longrightarrow\mathbb C[z,z^{-1},y],
\qquad x\longmapsto z+z^{-1},
\]
is injective. If a nonzero polynomial has degree \(d\) in \(x\), with leading coefficient \(c(y)\ne0\), its substituted Laurent polynomial has coefficient \(c(y)\) at \(z^d\), which cannot be canceled by terms of lower degree in \(x\).

For each \(k=1,\ldots,9\), we have \(b_k\ne0\): otherwise \(\zeta^{2k}=1\), requiring \(19\mid2k\). The discriminant of \(Q_k\), viewed as a quadratic in \(y\), is
\[
\Delta_k(x)=a_k^2x^2-4(x^2+b_k)
=b_k(x^2-4).
\]
It is not a square in \(\mathbb C(x)\), because it has a zero of order one at \(x=2\); every zero or pole of a rational square has even order. Over a field of characteristic zero, a quadratic is reducible exactly when its discriminant is a square, by the quadratic formula. Thus \(Q_k\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb C(x)\).

It follows that \(Q_k\) is irreducible in \(\mathbb C[x,y]\). A factorization with both factors of positive degree in \(y\) would contradict the preceding field irreducibility. A factor independent of \(y\) would have to divide the leading coefficient \(1\), so would be constant.

The nine quadratics are distinct. If \(a_k=a_\ell\), then \(\zeta^\ell\) must be one of the two roots \(\zeta^k,\zeta^{-k}\) of
\[
u^2-a_ku+1=0.
\]
Hence \(\ell\equiv k\) or \(\ell\equiv-k\pmod{19}\). For \(1\le k,\ell\le9\), this forces \(k=\ell\). Since the quadratics are monic in \(y\), they are also pairwise nonproportional. None equals the linear factor \(y-x\). Consequently, (1) gives ten distinct irreducible affine components.

We now verify the statement in \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\). Write
\[
x=X_0/X_1,\qquad y=Y_0/Y_1,
\]
and let
\[
p_h(U,V)=V^{19}p(U/V).
\]
The polynomial extends to the projective map
\[
[U:V]\longmapsto[p_h(U,V):V^{19}].
\]
This map is defined everywhere: when \(V\ne0\), its second coordinate is nonzero; when \(V=0\), monicity gives \(p_h(U,0)=U^{19}\ne0\).

Equality of the two projective values is therefore precisely
\[
p_h(Y_0,Y_1)X_1^{19}
-p_h(X_0,X_1)Y_1^{19}=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Bihomogenizing (1) factors its left side as
\[
(Y_0X_1-X_0Y_1)\prod_{k=1}^{9}\widetilde Q_k,
\]
where
\[
\widetilde Q_k=
X_1^2Y_0^2-a_kX_0X_1Y_0Y_1
+X_0^2Y_1^2+b_kX_1^2Y_1^2.
\tag{3}
\]

There are no additional boundary components. In (2), setting \(X_1=0\) forces \(Y_1=0\), and conversely. Thus the only point outside the affine chart is \((\infty,\infty)\), which already belongs to the diagonal.

For completeness, each equation (3) defines exactly the closure of its affine quadratic. Its only possible boundary point is likewise \((\infty,\infty)\), and that point belongs to the closure: the points
\[
\left(z+z^{-1},\ \zeta^kz+\zeta^{-k}z^{-1}\right)
\]
lie on \(Q_k=0\) and tend to \((\infty,\infty)\) as \(z\to\infty\). Algebraic closed sets over \(\mathbb C\) are closed in the usual topology, so this limit lies in the Zariski closure.

Each affine quadratic is irreducible, and the closure of an irreducible set remains irreducible: a decomposition of the closure into two closed subsets would restrict to such a decomposition of its dense affine part. The closures remain distinct because their affine intersections are distinct. Together with the diagonal, they exhaust (2). Hence \(X\) has exactly ten irreducible components over \(\mathbb C\).

The nine quadratic components are nonlinear. Their affine equations are irreducible quadratics and therefore cannot define affine lines. Their projective closures have bidegree \((2,2)\), as (3) shows, so they are also distinct from graphs of projective linear transformations, which have bidegree \((1,1)\). This proves the required component condition.

Finally, set \(v_n=D_n(19)\). Then
\[
v_0=2,\qquad v_1=19,\qquad
v_n=19v_{n-1}-v_{n-2}.
\]
Successive integer arithmetic gives
\[
v_{17}=5227260815326346060059,\qquad
v_{18}=99042070146811639444802.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(19)=v_{19}
&=19(99042070146811639444802)
-5227260815326346060059\\
&=1876572071974094803391179.
\end{aligned}
\]

## Proof

Define
\[
D_0(x)=2,\qquad D_1(x)=x,\qquad
D_n(x)=xD_{n-1}(x)-D_{n-2}(x)\quad(n\ge2),
\]
and take \(p=D_{19}\).

Induction shows that every \(D_n\) has integer coefficients, that \(D_n\) is monic of degree \(n\) for \(n\ge1\), and that
\[
D_n(-x)=(-1)^nD_n(x).
\]
Thus \(p\) is odd, monic, and has real coefficients.

The recurrence gives the formal generating function
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}D_n(x)t^n=\frac{2-xt}{1-xt+t^2}.
\]
Expanding the denominator as \(\sum_{m\ge0}(xt-t^2)^m\) and collecting coefficients yields, for \(n\ge1\),
\[
D_n(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
(-1)^j
\left[
\binom{n-j}{j}+\binom{n-j-1}{j-1}
\right]x^{n-2j},
\]
where a binomial coefficient with lower index \(-1\) is zero. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(x)={}&x^{19}-19x^{17}+152x^{15}-665x^{13}
+1729x^{11}\\
&-2717x^9+2508x^7-1254x^5+285x^3-19x.
\end{aligned}
\]
This establishes the coefficient list and the required linear coefficient \(-19\).

We prove that the equality curve has ten irreducible components. The recurrence first gives the Laurent-polynomial identity
\[
D_n(u+u^{-1})=u^n+u^{-n}.
\]
It holds for \(n=0,1\); multiplication by \(u+u^{-1}\) and subtraction of the preceding term proves the induction step.

Put
\[
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/19},\qquad
c_k=\frac{\zeta^k+\zeta^{-k}}2
=\cos\frac{2\pi k}{19}\quad(1\le k\le9),
\]
and define
\[
Q_k(x,y)=x^2+y^2-2c_kxy-4(1-c_k^2).
\]
We claim the polynomial identity
\[
p(y)-p(x)=(y-x)\prod_{k=1}^{9}Q_k(x,y). \tag{1}
\]

To prove it, let \(u\) be transcendental over \(\mathbb C\), set \(x=u+u^{-1}\), and work in \(\mathbb C(u)[y]\). The nineteen elements
\[
y_j=\zeta^ju+\zeta^{-j}u^{-1},
\qquad 0\le j\le18,
\]
are distinct: for different indices their difference is a nonzero Laurent polynomial in \(u\). Moreover,
\[
p(y_j)=(\zeta^ju)^{19}+(\zeta^ju)^{-19}
=u^{19}+u^{-19}=p(x).
\]
They therefore exhaust the roots of the monic degree-\(19\) polynomial \(p(y)-p(x)\). Since \(y_0=x\), pairing indices \(k\) and \(19-k\) gives
\[
y_k+y_{19-k}=2c_kx,\qquad
y_ky_{19-k}=x^2-4(1-c_k^2),
\]
which proves (1) after substitution.

This substitution proves an identity in \(\mathbb C[x,y]\): \(u+u^{-1}\) is transcendental over \(\mathbb C\), because otherwise the equation
\[
u^2-(u+u^{-1})u+1=0
\]
would make \(u\) algebraic over \(\mathbb C\). Substitution is consequently injective on \(\mathbb C[x,y]\).

Each \(Q_k\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb C\). As a monic quadratic in \(y\), its discriminant is
\[
\Delta_k(x)=4(1-c_k^2)(4-x^2).
\]
For \(1\le k\le9\), the angle \(2\pi k/19\) lies strictly between \(0\) and \(\pi\), so \(1-c_k^2\ne0\). Hence \(\Delta_k\) has simple zeros at \(x=2\) and \(x=-2\). A rational square has even order at every zero and pole, so \(\Delta_k\) is not a square in \(\mathbb C(x)\). The quadratic discriminant criterion therefore proves irreducibility in \(\mathbb C(x)[y]\). A factorization in \(\mathbb C[x,y]\) would either persist in that field or have a nonconstant factor depending only on \(x\); the latter is impossible because the leading coefficient in \(y\) is \(1\).

The \(c_k\) are distinct, since cosine is strictly decreasing on \((0,\pi)\). Thus (1) has ten distinct irreducible factors: the diagonal factor and nine irreducible quadratics.

We now verify the projective interpretation. Write
\[
x=[X:Z],\qquad y=[Y:W],\qquad
P(X,Z)=Z^{19}p(X/Z).
\]
The polynomial defines the everywhere-defined morphism
\[
[X:Z]\longmapsto[P(X,Z):Z^{19}],
\]
because \(P(X,0)=X^{19}\). Therefore its projective equality curve has equation
\[
P(Y,W)Z^{19}-P(X,Z)W^{19}=0. \tag{2}
\]
Bihomogenizing (1) factors (2) as
\[
(YZ-XW)\prod_{k=1}^{9}
\left(
X^2W^2+Y^2Z^2-2c_kXYZW
-4(1-c_k^2)Z^2W^2
\right).
\]
The bidegrees sum to \((1,1)+9(2,2)=(19,19)\), so no additional factor is introduced.

Each quadratic factor has only one point outside the affine chart \(ZW\ne0\), namely \((\infty,\infty)\): setting \(Z=0\) forces \(W=0\), and conversely. This point belongs to its affine curve’s closure, as witnessed by
\[
x=u+u^{-1},\qquad
y=\zeta^ku+\zeta^{-k}u^{-1}
\]
as \(u\to\infty\). Thus its projective zero set is precisely the closure of its irreducible affine curve, and is irreducible. The diagonal likewise closes to an irreducible component. The ten closures remain distinct because their affine parts are distinct.

Consequently, the projective equality curve has exactly ten irreducible components. The nine quadratic components are not linear: their affine defining polynomials are irreducible of total degree two, and their projective closures have bidegree \((2,2)\).

Finally, write \(p(x)=xR(x^2)\), where
\[

-2717t^4+2508t^3-1254t^2+285t-19.

\[
R(361)=98766951156531305441641.
\]
Hence
\[
p(19)=19R(361)=1876572071974094803391179.
\]
