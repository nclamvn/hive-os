C01
Let \(\Omega\) be either \(\mathbb T^2=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^2\), with volume one, or \(\mathbb R^2\), with Lebesgue measure. For \(0<\nu<e^{-1}\), consider the unforced incompressible Navier–Stokes equations
\[
\partial_tu^\nu+u^\nu\cdot\nabla u^\nu+\nabla\pi^\nu=\nu\Delta u^\nu,\qquad \nabla\cdot u^\nu=0,\qquad u^\nu(0)=u_0^\nu.
\]
Take distributional solutions belonging to \(L^\infty(0,S;L^2(\Omega;\mathbb R^2))\cap L^2(0,S;H^1(\Omega;\mathbb R^2))\) for every finite \(S>0\), attaining their initial data strongly in \(L^2\), and satisfying
\[
\frac12\|u^\nu(t)\|_2^2+\nu\int_0^t\|\nabla u^\nu(s)\|_2^2\,ds\leq\frac12\|u_0^\nu\|_2^2.
\]
Here \(H^1\) consists of square-integrable functions with square-integrable weak first derivatives, and all spatial norms are over \(\Omega\).

Write \(\omega^\nu=\partial_1u_2^\nu-\partial_2u_1^\nu\). For parameters \(1<p<\infty\) and \(M>0\), suppose the initial vorticities are finite signed measures admitting decompositions
\[
\omega_0^\nu=\mu_0^\nu+f_0^\nu,
\]
where \(\mu_0^\nu\) is a finite nonnegative measure and \(f_0^\nu\in L^1(\Omega)\cap L^p(\Omega)\), with
\[
\sup_{0<\nu<e^{-1}}\bigl(\|u_0^\nu\|_2+|\omega_0^\nu|(\Omega)+\|f_0^\nu\|_1+\|f_0^\nu\|_p\bigr)\leq M.
\]
The notation \(|\omega_0^\nu|(\Omega)\) denotes total variation. Initial data may depend arbitrarily on \(\nu\) subject to these bounds. Define
\[
D_\nu=\nu\int_{1/2}^{1}\|\omega^\nu(t)\|_2^2\,dt.
\]
Call \(q\geq0\) admissible if, for every \(p,M\), there exist constants \(C>0\) and \(\nu_0\in(0,e^{-1})\), depending only on \(q,p,M\), such that
\[
D_\nu\leq C[\log(1/\nu)]^{-q}\qquad(0<\nu<\nu_0)
\]
holds for both choices of \(\Omega\) and every family satisfying the assumptions. The logarithm is natural. What is the supremum of the admissible exponents?


C02

for which there exist \(u,v\in\mathbb{R}\) such that

C04
For every finite simple graph \(G=(V,E)\) with \(V\ne\varnothing\) and every integer \(r\), if \(D(G,r)=0\), then \(r\in\{0,-2\}\). Here a graph is simple if its edges are unordered pairs of distinct vertices, a subset \(S\subseteq V\) is dominating if every vertex in \(V\setminus S\) is adjacent to some vertex of \(S\), and its domination polynomial is
\[
D(G,x)=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq V\\ S\text{ is dominating}}}x^{|S|}.
\]


C05
For a positive integer $n$, let $A_{n}$ be the number of perfect powers less than or equal to $n$. Here, a perfect power is a number that can be expressed in the form $a^{b}$, where $a$ is a positive integer and $b$ is an integer greater than or equal to 2. Prove that there are infinitely many $n$ such that $A_{n}$ divides $n+2024$.

C06
There are 120 students in DeepMind highschool, and each student `loves' some of the students. Here `love' is not always a mutual relationship; a student A may love B while B may not love A, and it is also possible that a student loves oneself. For the set $F$ of all $2^{120}$ subsets of the students, define the function $f: F \rightarrow F$ as the function that maps each $X \in F$ to the set of students loved by one or more students in $X$. For $A, B \in F$, $A$ and $B$ are said to have a 'lovely relationship' iff there exists a natural number $k$ such that $f^{k}(A)=B$. Now we want to select distinct elements $A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots A_{t}$ from $F$ such that for any $1 \leq i<j \leq t$, $A_{i}$ and $A_{j}$ have a lovely relationship. Let $M(f)$ be the maximum possible value of $t$.  Show that $M(f) \leq 2^{70}$.
