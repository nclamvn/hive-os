PROBLEM
C05
For a positive integer $n$, let $A_{n}$ be the number of perfect powers less than or equal to $n$. Here, a perfect power is a number that can be expressed in the form $a^{b}$, where $a$ is a positive integer and $b$ is an integer greater than or equal to 2. Prove that there are infinitely many $n$ such that $A_{n}$ divides $n+2024$.

REPLAYED RESPONSE 1
**C05.** Let \(B(x)\) be the number of perfect powers at most \(x\) that are not squares. Every such number has a representation \(a^b\) with \(a\ge2\) and \(b\ge3\). Since \(2^b\le x\), we have \(b\le\log_2 x\). Thus, for \(x\ge2\),
\[
B(x)\le \sum_{b=3}^{\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor}\lfloor x^{1/b}\rfloor
\le x^{1/3}\log_2 x.
\]
In particular,
\[
B(M^2)\le 2M^{2/3}\log_2 M=o(M).
\]

Call a positive integer \(m\) **clean** if
\[
I_m=\{m^2,m^2+1,\ldots,(m+1)^2-1\}
\]
contains no perfect power other than \(m^2\). There are infinitely many clean integers: among the \(M-1\) disjoint intervals \(I_1,\ldots,I_{M-1}\), every interval that is not clean contains a distinct nonsquare perfect power at most \(M^2\). Consequently, at most \(B(M^2)=o(M)\) of these intervals are not clean.

For a clean \(m\), the value of \(A_n\) is constant throughout \(I_m\), equal to
\[
k=A_{m^2}=m+B(m^2),
\]
because precisely \(m\) positive squares are at most \(m^2\), including \(1\). The bound above gives
\[
k\le m+2m^{2/3}\log_2 m<2m+1
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\).

Choose any sufficiently large clean \(m\), and set
\[
n=k\left\lceil\frac{m^2+2024}{k}\right\rceil-2024.
\]
Then
\[
m^2\le n<m^2+k\le m^2+2m+1=(m+1)^2.
\]
Hence \(n\in I_m\), so \(A_n=k\), while the definition of \(n\) gives \(k\mid n+2024\).

There are infinitely many sufficiently large clean \(m\), and their intervals are disjoint, so they yield infinitely many distinct integers \(n\) with
\[
\boxed{A_n\mid n+2024}.
\]
\(\square\)

**C05.** The preceding solution is correct. In particular, \(1\) is counted among the squares, and any additional perfect power in an interval between consecutive squares must be a nonsquare. Here is a self-contained proof making those points explicit.

Let \(B(x)\) count the nonsquare perfect powers at most \(x\). Every such number has a representation \(a^b\) with \(a\ge2\) and \(b\ge3\). Since \(2^b\le x\), we obtain, for \(x\ge2\),
\[
B(x)\le \sum_{b=3}^{\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor}\lfloor x^{1/b}\rfloor
\le x^{1/3}\log_2 x.
\]
Overcounting numbers with multiple representations does not affect this upper bound. Consequently,
\[
B(M^2)\le 2M^{2/3}\log_2 M=o(M).
\]

Call \(m\) **clean** if the integer interval
\[
I_m=\{m^2,m^2+1,\ldots,(m+1)^2-1\}
\]
contains no perfect power other than \(m^2\). The only square in \(I_m\) is \(m^2\), so every interval that is not clean contains a nonsquare perfect power.

The intervals \(I_1,\ldots,I_{M-1}\) are disjoint and lie below \(M^2\). Selecting one nonsquare perfect power from each interval that is not clean therefore gives distinct numbers. Thus at least
\[
M-1-B(M^2)=M-o(M)
\]
of these intervals are clean. This quantity tends to infinity, proving that there are infinitely many clean \(m\).

For a clean \(m\), \(A_n\) is constant throughout \(I_m\), with value
\[
k=A_{m^2}=m+B(m^2).
\]
Here the \(m\) squares include \(1=1^2\). The estimate above gives
\[
k=m+o(m)<2m+1
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\).

n=k\left\lceil\frac{m^2+2024}{k}\right\rceil-2024.

Using \(t\le\lceil t\rceil<t+1\), we have
\[
m^2\le n<m^2+k<(m+1)^2.

\[
n+2024=k\left\lceil\frac{m^2+2024}{k}\right\rceil,

and therefore \(A_n\mid n+2024\).

Infinitely many clean \(m\) are sufficiently large, and their disjoint intervals yield distinct \(n\). Thus
\[
\boxed{\text{There are infinitely many }n\text{ such that }A_n\mid n+2024.}
