**C05.** Let \(B(x)\) denote the number of perfect powers at most \(x\) that are not squares. Each such number has a representation \(a^b\) with \(a\ge2\) and \(b\ge3\). Necessarily \(b\le\log_2 x\), so
\[
B(x)\le\sum_{b=3}^{\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor}\lfloor x^{1/b}\rfloor
\le x^{1/3}\log_2 x.
\]
Repeated representations only cause overcounting, which is harmless. In particular,
\[
B(M^2)\le 2M^{2/3}\log_2 M=o(M).
\]

Call a positive integer \(m\) *clean* if
\[
I_m=\{m^2,m^2+1,\ldots,(m+1)^2-1\}
\]
contains no perfect power other than \(m^2\). The only square in \(I_m\) is \(m^2\); therefore, every interval that is not clean contains a nonsquare perfect power.

The intervals \(I_1,\ldots,I_{M-1}\) are disjoint and lie below \(M^2\). Consequently, at most \(B(M^2)\) of them are not clean, and at least
\[
M-1-B(M^2)=M-o(M)
\]
are clean. This tends to infinity, so there are infinitely many clean \(m\).

For a clean \(m\), the number \(A_n\) is constant throughout \(I_m\), with value
\[
k=A_{m^2}=m+B(m^2).
\]
Indeed, the squares at most \(m^2\) are \(1^2,\ldots,m^2\), including \(1\), and all remaining perfect powers are counted by \(B(m^2)\). Our estimate gives
\[
k=m+o(m)<2m+1
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\).

For any sufficiently large clean \(m\), set
\[
n=k\left\lceil\frac{m^2+2024}{k}\right\rceil-2024.
\]
The ceiling inequality \(t\le\lceil t\rceil<t+1\) yields
\[
m^2\le n<m^2+k<(m+1)^2.
\]
Thus \(n\in I_m\) and \(A_n=k\). By construction, \(k\mid n+2024\), hence
\[
A_n\mid n+2024.
\]

Infinitely many clean \(m\) are sufficiently large, and their disjoint intervals produce distinct \(n\). This proves the assertion. \(\square\)
