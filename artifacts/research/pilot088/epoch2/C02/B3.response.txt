**C02.** The audited draft establishes
\[
\boxed{30\le M\le36},
\]
where \(M\) is the largest possible cardinality. The proof below is rigorous, but **the exact maximum remains unresolved**.

For the upper bound, project every tuple onto its first two coordinates. This projection is injective, since two distinct tuples with the same projection would agree in two positions. Thus
\[
M\le6^2=36.
\]

For the lower bound, work over \(\mathbb F_5\), using the symbols \(0,1,2,3,4\). Let
\[
D=\{(x,y,x+y,x+2y,x+3y):x,y\in\mathbb F_5\}.
\]
The coefficient vectors of these five coordinates are
\[
(1,0),\ (0,1),\ (1,1),\ (1,2),\ (1,3).
\]
Every two are linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_5\). Consequently, agreement in any two coordinates determines \(x,y\), so distinct tuples in \(D\) agree in at most one position.

Index the five positions by \(\mathbb F_5\). Remove the five tuples
\[
T_t=(t,t,2t,3t,4t),\qquad t\in\mathbb F_5.
\]
For each \(t\), insert two tuples:

- \(U_t\), obtained from \(T_t\) by replacing the entries in positions \(t-1,t+1\) with \(5\);
- \(V_t\), obtained from \(T_t\) by replacing the entries in positions \(t-2,t+2\) with \(5\).

All position arithmetic is modulo \(5\). We verify the agreement condition.

Two retained tuples already satisfy it. A retained tuple and a new tuple also satisfy it: the retained tuple differs from the corresponding \(T_t\), and replacing entries of \(T_t\) with \(5\) cannot create agreements with a tuple containing no \(5\).

For fixed \(t\), the replacement sets
\[
\{t-1,t+1\},\qquad\{t-2,t+2\}
\]
are disjoint and cover all positions except \(t\). Therefore \(U_t\) and \(V_t\) agree exactly in position \(t\).

For distinct \(t,s\), the original tuples \(T_t,T_s\) differ in every coordinate, because each coordinate is a nonzero multiple of the parameter. Their modified tuples can therefore agree only where both have the entry \(5\).

Finally, all ten replacement sets are distinct. Every unordered pair \(\{a,b\}\) of different positions has the unique midpoint
\[
t=(a+b)/2,
\]
and its offsets from that midpoint are either \(\{\pm1\}\) or \(\{\pm2\}\). Hence distinct replacement sets intersect in at most one position. This verifies the condition for new tuples associated with different parameters.

The inserted tuples are distinct from one another and from the retained tuples. The construction therefore has
\[
25-5+10=30
\]
members.

**Unresolved gap:** Neither this proof nor the critic’s audit excludes sizes \(31,\ldots,36\), or constructs a larger optimal set. Thus \(30\) is a proved attainable size, not a proved maximum; presenting it as the exact answer would be unjustified.
