PROBLEM
C06
There are 120 students in DeepMind highschool, and each student `loves' some of the students. Here `love' is not always a mutual relationship; a student A may love B while B may not love A, and it is also possible that a student loves oneself. For the set $F$ of all $2^{120}$ subsets of the students, define the function $f: F \rightarrow F$ as the function that maps each $X \in F$ to the set of students loved by one or more students in $X$. For $A, B \in F$, $A$ and $B$ are said to have a 'lovely relationship' iff there exists a natural number $k$ such that $f^{k}(A)=B$. Now we want to select distinct elements $A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots A_{t}$ from $F$ such that for any $1 \leq i<j \leq t$, $A_{i}$ and $A_{j}$ have a lovely relationship. Let $M(f)$ be the maximum possible value of $t$.  Show that $M(f) \leq 2^{70}$. 

REPLAYED RESPONSE 1
**C06.** Regard the students as vertices of a directed graph, with an edge \(u\to v\) when \(u\) loves \(v\). Then \(f^m(X)\) consists exactly of the vertices reachable from \(X\) by a walk of length \(m\).

We prove that every orbit of \(f\) contains fewer than \(2^{70}\) distinct sets. Put \(n=120\).

For each strongly connected component containing a directed cycle, let its **period** be the gcd of the lengths of its directed cycles. Let \(L\) be the least common multiple of these periods, taking \(L=1\) if there are no such components.

First, we bound \(L\). A component of size \(s\) has period at most \(s\), since it contains a cycle of length at most \(s\). Thus the sum of all component periods is at most \(120\).

Write \(L\) as a product of its maximal prime-power factors \(q_1,\ldots,q_r\). Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r q_i\le120.
\]
Indeed, splitting an integer into its prime-power factors cannot increase their sum: for integers \(a,b\ge2\), \(a+b\le ab\). Apply this to each component period, then retain only the largest power of each prime.

For every integer \(q\ge2\), except \(q=3\), we have \(q\le2^{q/2}\). At most one \(q_i\) equals \(3\). Consequently,
\[
L=\prod_iq_i
\le \frac{3}{2^{3/2}}\,2^{60}<2^{61}.
\tag{1}
\]

We next establish a uniform bound on when walk lengths become periodic.

**Claim.** In a strongly connected component of size \(s\) and period \(d\), for any vertices \(u,v\), all \(u\)-to-\(v\) walk lengths have one residue modulo \(d\), and every integer in that residue which is at least \(4s^2+2s\) occurs.

Fix a vertex \(r\). Choose shortest paths from \(r\) to each vertex \(x\), and from \(x\) to \(r\), of lengths \(p_x,q_x\le s-1\). Consider the positive lengths among
\[
p_x+q_x,\qquad p_x+1+q_y\quad(x\to y).
\]
These are lengths of closed walks based at \(r\), and are at most \(2s\). Their gcd equals \(d\). To see this, every closed walk decomposes into directed cycles, so \(d\) divides all the displayed lengths. Conversely, their gcd \(h\) divides
\[
(p_x+1+q_y)-(p_y+q_y)=p_x+1-p_y.
\]
Summing around any directed cycle shows that \(h\) divides its length. Hence \(h=d\).

We use the following elementary observation: if positive integers at most \(B\) have gcd \(d\), every multiple of \(d\) at least \(B^2\) is a nonnegative integer combination of them. After dividing by \(d\), let \(a\) be the smallest generator. Addition of the generators reaches every residue modulo \(a\), since their gcd is \(1\). A shortest sequence reaching a residue uses at most \(a-1\) additions, so its sum is at most
\[
(a-1)B/d\le (B/d)^2.
\]
Any larger integer in that residue is obtained by adding copies of \(a\). This proves the observation.

Concatenating the closed walks above therefore gives a closed walk at \(r\) of every length divisible by \(d\) and at least \(4s^2\). Attach a shortest path \(u\to r\) and a shortest path \(r\to v\); their combined length \(b\) is at most \(2s-2\). Thus every length congruent to \(b\pmod d\) and at least \(4s^2+2s\) occurs.

Finally, all \(u\)-to-\(v\) walks have that same residue: append any fixed \(v\)-to-\(u\) path to two such walks. Both resulting closed walks have lengths divisible by \(d\), so the original lengths differ by a multiple of \(d\). The claim follows.

Set
\[
T=4n^2+2n,\qquad K=n(T+L)+n.
\]
We show that, for any vertices \(u,v\) and every \(m\ge K\),
\[
\text{a walk of length \(m\) from \(u\) to \(v\) exists}
\iff
\text{one of length \(m+L\) exists}.
\tag{2}
\]

A walk visits at most \(n\) strongly connected components, and cannot return to a component after leaving it. At most \(n-1\) edges join different components. Therefore any walk of length at least \(K\) has a segment inside one component of length at least \(T+L\). Such a component necessarily contains a directed cycle.

Its period divides \(L\). By the claim, this segment can be replaced by a segment with the same endpoints whose length is increased by \(L\), or decreased by \(L\): both new lengths have the required residue and are at least \(T\). Starting with a length-\(m\) walk gives a length-\(m+L\) walk. Starting with a length-\(m+L\) walk gives a length-\(m\) walk. This proves (2).

It follows that, for every subset \(X\),
\[
f^{m+L}(X)=f^m(X)\qquad(m\ge K).
\]
Thus its forward orbit contains at most \(K+L\) distinct sets. Using (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
K+L
&=nT+n+(n+1)L\\
&<6{,}940{,}920+121\cdot2^{61}\\
&<2^{68}<2^{70}.
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(A_1,\ldots,A_t\) satisfy the required condition, every \(A_j\) belongs to the forward orbit of \(A_1\), because \(f^{k_j}(A_1)=A_j\) for some \(k_j\). Their distinctness therefore gives
\[
\boxed{M(f)\le2^{70}}.
