**C06.** Represent the students by a directed graph on \(n=120\) vertices, with \(u\to v\) when \(u\) loves \(v\). Then \(f^m(X)\) consists exactly of the vertices reachable from \(X\) by walks of length \(m\). We will bound the number of distinct sets in every forward orbit.

For each strongly connected component containing a directed cycle, let its period be the gcd of its directed cycle lengths. Let \(L\) be the lcm of these periods, with \(L=1\) if there are no such components.

A component of size \(s\) has period at most \(s\), since it contains a cycle of length at most \(s\). Hence the sum of all component periods is at most \(120\).

Let \(q_1,\ldots,q_r\) be the maximal prime-power factors of \(L\), one for each prime dividing \(L\). Then
\[
\sum_i q_i\le120.
\]
Indeed, splitting each component period into its prime-power factors cannot increase its sum, because \(a+b\le ab\) for integers \(a,b\ge2\). Retaining only the largest power of each prime further decreases the sum.

For every integer \(q\ge2\), except \(q=3\), we have \(q\le2^{q/2}\). This holds directly for \(2,4,5\), and then follows by induction in steps of two using \(q+2\le2q\). At most one \(q_i\) equals \(3\), so
\[
L=\prod_iq_i
\le \frac{3}{2^{3/2}}\,2^{60}<2^{61}.
\tag{1}
\]

We next prove an elementary walk-length bound.

**Lemma.** If positive integers at most \(B\) have gcd \(d\), every multiple of \(d\) at least \(B^2\) is a nonnegative integer combination of them.

Divide the integers by \(d\), and let \(a\) be the smallest resulting generator. The residues modulo \(a\) obtainable by nonnegative sums form a subgroup: a subset of a finite group containing zero and closed under addition also contains additive inverses. Since the normalized generators have gcd \(1\), Bézout’s identity implies that this subgroup is all of \(\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z\).

A shortest sequence of additions reaching any residue has no repeated intermediate residue, so uses at most \(a-1\) additions. Its sum is therefore at most
\[
C=(a-1)\frac Bd\le\left(\frac Bd\right)^2.
\]
Any integer at least \(C\) can be represented by choosing such a sum in its residue and adding copies of \(a\). If \(N\ge B^2\) is divisible by \(d\), then
\[
\frac Nd\ge\frac{B^2}{d}\ge\left(\frac Bd\right)^2\ge C.
\]
Multiplying the resulting representation by \(d\) proves the lemma.

**Claim.** In a strongly connected component of size \(s\) and period \(d\), all walks between fixed vertices have one residue modulo \(d\), and every integer in that residue at least \(4s^2+2s\) occurs.

Fix a vertex \(r\). Choose shortest paths \(r\to x\) and \(x\to r\), of lengths \(p_x,q_x\le s-1\). The positive lengths among
\[
p_x+q_x,\qquad p_x+1+q_y\quad(x\to y)
\]
are lengths of closed walks at \(r\), all at most \(2s\).

Their gcd is \(d\). Every closed walk decomposes into directed cycles, so \(d\) divides all these lengths. Conversely, their gcd \(h\) divides
\[
(p_x+1+q_y)-(p_y+q_y)=p_x+1-p_y.
\]
This also holds when the second length is zero. Summing around any directed cycle shows that \(h\) divides its length, proving \(h=d\).

The lemma with \(B=2s\), together with concatenation of these closed walks, gives a closed walk at \(r\) of every length divisible by \(d\) and at least \(4s^2\). Attach shortest paths \(u\to r\) and \(r\to v\), whose combined length \(b\) is at most \(2s-2\). Thus every integer congruent to \(b\pmod d\) and at least \(4s^2+2s\) occurs as a walk length from \(u\) to \(v\).

Finally, append a fixed \(v\to u\) path to any two \(u\to v\) walks. The resulting closed walks have lengths divisible by \(d\), so the original lengths differ by a multiple of \(d\). This proves the claim.

Set
\[
T=4n^2+2n=57{,}840,\qquad K=n(T+L)+n.
\]
We show that for every \(m\ge K\) and all vertices \(u,v\),
\[
\text{a length-\(m\) walk from \(u\) to \(v\) exists}
\iff
\text{a length-\((m+L)\) walk exists}.
\tag{2}
\]

A walk visits at most \(n\) strongly connected components and cannot return to one after leaving it. At most \(n-1\) edges join different components. Consequently, a walk of length at least \(K\) has a segment inside some component of length at least \(T+L\). Such a long segment repeats a vertex, so its component contains a cycle.

That component’s period divides \(L\). The claim allows replacement of the segment, preserving its endpoints, by one whose length is increased or decreased by \(L\): both replacement lengths are at least \(T\), and the residue modulo the period is unchanged. Increase a segment in a length-\(m\) walk, or decrease one in a length-\((m+L)\) walk, to establish (2).

Therefore, for every subset \(X\),
\[
f^{m+L}(X)=f^m(X)\qquad(m\ge K).
\]
Its forward orbit contains at most \(K+L\) distinct sets. By (1),
\[
K+L
=6{,}940{,}920+121L
<6{,}940{,}920+121\cdot2^{61}
<2^{68},
\]
where the last inequality follows from
\[
2^{68}-121\cdot2^{61}=7\cdot2^{61}>6{,}940{,}920.
\]

For any admissible sequence \(A_1,\ldots,A_t\), every \(A_j\) belongs to the forward orbit of \(A_1\), by the condition for \(i=1\). Since the sets are distinct,
\[
t\le K+L<2^{68}<2^{70}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{M(f)\le2^{70}}.
\]
