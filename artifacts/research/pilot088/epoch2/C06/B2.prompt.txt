PROBLEM
C06
There are 120 students in DeepMind highschool, and each student `loves' some of the students. Here `love' is not always a mutual relationship; a student A may love B while B may not love A, and it is also possible that a student loves oneself. For the set $F$ of all $2^{120}$ subsets of the students, define the function $f: F \rightarrow F$ as the function that maps each $X \in F$ to the set of students loved by one or more students in $X$. For $A, B \in F$, $A$ and $B$ are said to have a 'lovely relationship' iff there exists a natural number $k$ such that $f^{k}(A)=B$. Now we want to select distinct elements $A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots A_{t}$ from $F$ such that for any $1 \leq i<j \leq t$, $A_{i}$ and $A_{j}$ have a lovely relationship. Let $M(f)$ be the maximum possible value of $t$.  Show that $M(f) \leq 2^{70}$. 

REPLAYED RESPONSE 1
**C06.** Regard the students as vertices of a directed graph, with an edge \(u\to v\) when \(u\) loves \(v\). Then \(f^m(A)\) consists of the endpoints of walks of length \(m\) starting in \(A\).

We will prove that these sets become periodic, with bounds sufficient for the result.

First, we need an elementary fact about sums of integers.

**Lemma.** If positive integers \(b_1,\dots,b_r\) have greatest common divisor \(d\) and are all at most \(L\), then every multiple of \(d\) that is at least \(L^2\) is a nonnegative integer combination of the \(b_i\).

**Proof.** Divide the integers by \(d\), obtaining integers of gcd \(1\), maximum \(B\le L/d\), and minimum \(a\). Modulo \(a\), these integers generate every residue: the residues reachable by nonnegative sums form a subgroup of the finite additive group, and their gcd with \(a\) is \(1\).

For each residue, a shortest sequence of additions reaching it has at most \(a-1\) terms, since otherwise a residue repeats. Thus that residue has a representative sum at most \((a-1)B\le B^2\). Every integer at least \(B^2\) can consequently be represented by adding copies of \(a\) to the representative of its residue.

If \(x\ge L^2\) is divisible by \(d\), then
\[
x/d\ge L^2/d\ge (L/d)^2\ge B^2,
\]
so the assertion follows. \(\square\)

Consider a strongly connected component containing a directed cycle, with \(s\) vertices. Let \(g\) be the gcd of its directed cycle lengths. We claim that, for any vertices \(u,v\) in this component, all sufficiently large walk lengths from \(u\) to \(v\) are precisely one residue class modulo \(g\). A uniform sufficient threshold, for our graph, is
\[
T=4(120)^2+2(120)=57\,840.
\]

To prove this, fix a vertex \(r\). For every vertex \(v\), choose paths \(r\to v\) and \(v\to r\), of lengths \(a_v,b_v\le s-1\), with \(a_r=b_r=0\). The following are lengths of closed walks at \(r\):
\[
a_v+b_v,\qquad a_u+1+b_v\quad\text{for every edge }u\to v.
\]
Ignore any zero length. All these lengths are at most \(2s-1\), and their gcd is \(g\). Indeed, \(g\) divides them because every closed walk decomposes into directed cycles. Conversely, if \(d\) is their gcd, then for each edge \(u\to v\),
\[
d\mid (a_u+1+b_v)-(a_v+b_v)=a_u+1-a_v.
\]
Summing around any directed cycle shows that \(d\) divides its length. Hence \(d=g\).

The lemma now shows that every multiple of \(g\) at least \((2s-1)^2\) occurs as a closed-walk length at \(r\), by concatenating the chosen closed walks.

There is a walk \(u\to r\to v\) of some length \(h\le2s-2\). All walks \(u\to v\) have lengths congruent to \(h\) modulo \(g\): append a fixed walk \(v\to u\) and compare the resulting closed walks. Conversely, whenever
\[
m\equiv h\pmod g,\qquad m\ge T,
\]
the difference \(m-h\) is a multiple of \(g\) exceeding \((2s-1)^2\). Insert the corresponding closed walk at \(r\). This proves the claim.

Let \(g_1,\dots,g_q\) be the periods of all components containing cycles, and put
\[
P=\operatorname{lcm}(g_1,\dots,g_q),
\]
taking \(P=1\) if there are no such components. Each \(g_i\) is at most the size of its component, since it divides a simple cycle length. Therefore
\[
\sum_i g_i\le120.
\]

We next bound \(P\). For every positive integer \(d\ne3\),
\[
d\le2^{d/2}.
\]
In taking a product divisible by \(P\), retain all the \(g_i\), except that repeated occurrences of \(3\) may be discarded. If no \(3\) remains, this gives \(P\le2^{60}\). If one remains, it gives
\[
P\le3\cdot2^{117/2}<2^{61}.
\]
Thus, in all cases, \(P\le2^{61}\).

Set
\[
N=120(T+P).
\]
We claim that for every pair of vertices \(u,v\) and every \(m\ge N\),
\[
\text{a length-\(m\) walk \(u\to v\) exists}
\iff
\text{a length-\(m+P\) walk \(u\to v\) exists}.
\]

Any walk visits each strongly connected component at most once after leaving it. It therefore consists of at most \(120\) internal segments, separated by at most \(119\) edges. If its total length is at least \(N\), some internal segment has length at least \(T+P\); otherwise its length would be at most
\[
120(T+P-1)+119=N-1.
\]
That segment lies in a component containing a cycle. Its length can be increased or decreased by \(P\), preserving its endpoints: both lengths remain at least \(T\), and \(P\) is divisible by that component’s period.

Starting with a length-\(m\) walk, increase such a segment by \(P\). Starting with a length-\(m+P\) walk, decrease such a segment by \(P\). This proves the equivalence.

Consequently, for every subset \(A\),
\[
f^{m+P}(A)=f^m(A)\qquad(m\ge N).
\]
Its forward orbit thus contains at most \(N+P\) distinct subsets.

Finally, in any selection \(A_1,\dots,A_t\) satisfying the problem’s condition, every \(A_j\) belongs to the forward orbit of \(A_1\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
M(f)&\le N+P\\
&=6\,940\,800+121P\\
&\le6\,940\,800+121\cdot2^{61}\\
&<128\cdot2^{61}=2^{68}<2^{70}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This establishes the required bound. \(\square\)
