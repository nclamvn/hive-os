**C06. The proof is sound.** It establishes the stronger bound \(M(f)<2^{68}\). There are no substantive errors or unresolved gaps.

The main steps withstand scrutiny:

- **Integer-sum lemma.** Nonnegative sums modulo \(a\) form a subgroup because any additive submonoid of a finite group is a subgroup. The gcd assumption makes this subgroup all of \(\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z\). In a shortest sequence reaching a residue, the successive partial residues cannot repeat; otherwise the intervening terms could be removed. Thus at most \(a-1\) terms suffice. The representative bound and subsequent rescaling by \(d\) are correct.

- **Period of a strongly connected component.** The displayed closed-walk lengths have gcd exactly \(g\). Ignoring zero lengths causes no difficulty: every integer divides zero, so the subtraction argument remains valid when \(a_v+b_v=0\). Summing
  \[
  a_u+1-a_v
  \]
  around a cycle telescopes to its length. Concatenating closed walks at \(r\) realizes every nonnegative combination of the generators. Consequently the claimed eventual residue-class description of walk lengths follows. The threshold is sufficient, since
  \[
  (2s-1)^2+(2s-2)\le239^2+238=57\,359<57\,840=T.
  \]

- **Bound on \(P\).** Removing repeated entries equal to \(3\) preserves the lcm. The retained product is therefore divisible by \(P\). The inequality \(d\le2^{d/2}\) holds for every positive integer except \(3\): check \(1,2,4\), then induct for \(d\ge4\) using \((d+1)/d\le5/4<\sqrt2\). If a \(3\) remains, the other retained entries sum to at most \(117\), giving
  \[
  P\le3\,2^{117/2}<2^{61}.
  \]

- **Uniform periodicity for arbitrary walks.** A walk cannot return to a strongly connected component after leaving it, since that would merge the components. Hence it has at most \(120\) internal segments and \(119\) connecting edges. The pigeonhole bound correctly forces a segment of length at least \(T+P\). Increasing or decreasing that segment by \(P\) preserves its endpoints, and the decreased length remains at least \(T\). This proves both directions of the stated equivalence.

Finally, periodicity from index \(N\) means every forward orbit has at most \(N+P\) distinct sets, represented by indices \(0,\dots,N+P-1\). The selection condition places every \(A_j\) in the orbit of \(A_1\), so
\[
M(f)\le6\,940\,800+121P
\le6\,940\,800+121\cdot2^{61}
<2^{68}<2^{70}.
\]
The argument also works whether “natural number” includes zero.
