## Exact factor-partition scope

Let h=2ell+1 be prime with ell an odd prime, T a positive integer coprime to h, and T=product q_i^e_i. Signed exponent z_i lies in [-e_i,e_i]. It corresponds exactly to coprime positive u=product q_i^max(z_i,0), v=product q_i^max(-z_i,0), with uv dividing T. Unused exponent e_i-|z_i| belongs to k=T/(uv), never a hidden shared prime in u and v. The ratio image contains -1 iff some such pair obeys h dividing u+v.

The signed image is not the mere subgroup generated by factors: finite exponent boxes matter. Repetitions contribute valuation mass W=sum e_i for q_i not congruent to 1 mod h, not the number of distinct prime factors. Factors congruent to 1 contribute only unused/value mass, not group growth. A factor congruent to -1 gives a hit immediately; all-quadratic-residue factors cannot hit -1.

For an admissible prime p, h=3 mod4, p not dividing h and T=(p+h)/4, let A=(u+v)/h. The known Type-II tuple is (T, A p u k, A p v k). Its exact identity follows from u+v=A h and 4T=p+h. T is not divisible by p; the other two entries are. Coprimality and h>=7 exclude u=v, so after sorting denominators are strict and exactly two are p-divisible. These facts require the stated p/h/integrality domain, not just a residue hit. This is the existing [Tao/Vaughan parametrization, Proposition 1.2 and Remark 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1107.1010v2), not a new universal construction.

## Threshold and inverse-boundary proof challenge

Use additive logs in cyclic G of order 2ell. The target -1 has log ell. A nonidentity QR has nonzero even log/order ell; a QNR other than -1 has odd primitive log/order 2ell. Represent each valuation unit by a virtual atom {0,a,-a}. The sum of repeated atoms equals the original capped-exponent image: cancellation changes the net exponent and increases unused mass. It does not authorize using one prime simultaneously in coprime u and v.

Assume the target is missing and some QNR is present. Its final-image stabilizer must be trivial. A stabilizer of order 2 contains ell and, since 0 is in the image, would imply the target is present. A stabilizer of order ell plus an odd-log atom supplies both cosets and hence the full group. Full stabilizer likewise hits. The -1 atom is already excluded by immediate success.

Every intermediate sumset has stabilizer contained in the final stabilizer, because a period persists under further addition. Therefore all are aperiodic in the missing-target case. Iterating [Kneser's addition theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.3539v1) gives size at least 2W+1. W>=ell contradicts the group order 2ell. This proves the generic sufficient threshold. It is an elementary application of a known theorem, not a new milestone by itself; previous project A19 work must be credited.

The threshold cannot uniformly decrease to ell-1: h=7, T=3^2=9 has W=2 and a QNR factor, but signed image {1,2,3,4,5} misses 6. Here p=4T-h=29 is genuinely prime, although outside the residual campaign. T=27 reaches W=3 and hits through u=27,v=1.

At W=ell-1 with a QNR, the proposed inverse classification is valid: **a miss occurs iff every nonidentity factor residue belongs to one inverse pair {r,r^-1}, where r is a primitive QNR.** Independent proof details:

1. If both nonzero even and odd logs occur, group those two atoms first. The even portion has three points. In the odd coset, pass to the prime-order quotient of order ell: the sums of two and three distinct points have at least min(ell,4) points. For ell>=5, the initial sum has at least seven points; subsequent atoms add at least two each. Thus a miss would require 2W+3<=2ell-1, or W<=ell-2. At ell=3, the odd coset is already full and includes the target. This is the existing mixed-parity obstruction, not recycled as new research.
2. If two primitive odd logs a,b are not equal up to sign, their nine sums i a+j b for i,j in {-1,0,1} are distinct. A collision would have coefficient differences of magnitude at most two. Mixed coefficient parity cannot vanish modulo even 2ell; coefficient magnitude two on both sides reduces to a=±b modulo ell. Since a±b is even and ell odd, the possible extra ell offset is impossible, leaving only the excluded equality up to sign. Zero-coordinate collisions cannot occur for primitive logs. Hence later growth gives 2W+5<=2ell-1 for a miss, or W<=ell-3. At ell=3 there is no pair of noninverse primitive odd directions.
3. Consequently W=ell-1 missing the target forces no even direction and a single primitive odd direction up to sign. Conversely, if all atoms are ±a with a primitive odd log, their image is [-W,W]a. At W=ell-1 this has exactly 2ell-1 points and omits only ell: multiplication by odd a fixes the unique order-two target. This proves both directions, with repeated and unused mass retained.

A target-sensitive corollary is W>=ell-2 sufficient when two QNR directions are noninverse. It is not claimed universal, novel or necessary for every hit. The group bound W<=ell-3 is sharp abstractly at ell=5: h=11, residues 2 and 7 produce {1,...,9}, missing 10 at W=2. Taking actual T=14 gives p=45 composite, so that example is a group control, not an admissible prime-target witness.

Another valid but standard routing corollary: if nonidentity QR valuation mass W_QR>=(ell-1)/2 and a QNR factor exists, the QR-only image fills the subgroup of order ell, and multiplying a QNR supplies the odd coset. For example h=11,T=18,p=61 yields u=9,v=2. This is a Cauchy–Davenport/Kneser mechanism, not a new root bridge.
