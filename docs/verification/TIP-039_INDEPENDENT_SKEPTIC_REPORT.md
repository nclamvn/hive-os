## 1. Independently challenged proof

Let G be a finite abelian group, t a nonidentity involution, and S the full
signed product box of factors r_i with positive valuations e_i. Assume t not
in S. Write H=Stab(S), with exact stabilizer, and n=|G/H|. Identity belongs
to S, so H is a subset of S; hence t is not in H. The coset tH therefore
has exact order2, and n is even. No cyclicity or unique involution is needed.

The image S/H is aperiodic: any nontrivial period would lift to a subgroup
strictly larger than H stabilizing S. Periods of partial product boxes persist
when subsequent boxes are multiplied. Thus every partial quotient sum is also
aperiodic. For a visible r_i not in H, quotient order d_i<=2e_i+1 would make
its entire interval [-e_i,e_i]r_i fill the cyclic subgroup it generates. That
nontrivial subgroup would stabilize the final quotient box, a contradiction.
Consequently d_i>2e_i+1, and each visible atom has exactly2e_i+1 elements.
Iterated aperiodic Kneser gives |S/H|>=1+2V, V=sum of visible valuations.
Since tH is missing, |S/H|<=n-1. Therefore2V<=n-2.

In particular a visible quotient-order2 factor cannot survive at the **exact**
stabilizer; a weighted order2 workaround would introduce an inconsistent atom
rather than strengthen this proof. A generated subgroup or chosen proper
subgroup is not a substitute for exact H.

Composite G can have multiple involutions and even H. Do not import H odd,
n2mod4, QNR visibility or QNR-index>=6 from prime3mod4 groups. The standard
[Kneser theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.3539v1) supports the above deduction;
it is not a new root bridge or novelty certificate.
