## 2. Root reciprocity and the asymptotic supply corollary

For distinct odd primes p=1 mod4 and h=3 mod4 and an odd prime q dividing
T=(p+h)/4, q differs from p and h. Since p=-h modq, quadratic reciprocity
cancels the two signs and gives `(q/h)=(p/q)`. For q=2, the right side is
the supplementary quadratic character, not an ordinary Legendre symbol.
When 2 divides T, p=-h mod8 proves the same equality. Residual p=1 mod840
makes 2,3,5,7 split. These statements concern each factor, not the total
character of T.

Primary statements inspected:

- [Banks–Guo, v1, Introduction (1.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.05523v1)
  states Burgess's least-nonresidue bound for every fixed positive epsilon.
  Its arXiv abstract and PDF first-page body were also checked for identity
  and the stated exponent; this is not a complete reproof of Burgess.
- [Xylouris, Acta Arithmetica 150.1 (2011), pp.65–66](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83078)
  defines P(a,M) for reduced progressions and states Theorem1.1 with exponent
  5.18 and an effectively computable constant. The theorem statement and
  hypotheses were checked, not every numerical estimate in its proof.

The least positive nonresidue q is prime: otherwise every proper prime factor
is smaller, has character +1 by minimality, and their product cannot have
character -1. Also q<p. Fix epsilon=0.16-1/(4sqrt(e)), approximately
0.008367335071841647>0. Then q<=C_epsilon p^0.16. The progression
a=-p mod4q is reduced and a=3 mod4. Xylouris yields a prime h in it with
h<=C_L(4q)^5.18<=C' p^0.8288; weakening to5.2 gives the registered0.832.
The constants are independent of p. Therefore h<p for all sufficiently large
p, q divides T, and `(q/h)=-1`.

Verdict: **valid derived classical eventual supply corollary**. No explicit
constant or finite cutoff is extracted here. It does not admit any finite
root, ensure `(p/h)=-1`, supply enough valuation, eliminate quotient
obstructions, pay split coverage or produce a Type-II witness. Other inert
valuations may make T's total character positive. No novelty is established.

## 3. Inverse-direction lemma and constructive boundary

Write G=U(h), and let L be generated by split residues. Since h=3 mod4,
the quadratic-residue subgroup has odd order; hence L has odd order. With no
inert factor, the entire legal image is in L and cannot contain -1.

Otherwise fix the smallest inert prime's coset aL. Its order d is even and
2 mod4; the unique involution in G/L is both (-1)L and a^(d/2)L. This does
not require d=[G:L]. If every inert factor coset is aL or its inverse,
independent legal signed intervals add to the exact interval [-E,E], even
when direction assignments differ. For d=2, both assignments coincide and
the registered +1 tie is legitimate. Consequently E<d/2 proves a local
miss. E>=d/2 proves only a projected hit.

A split-box representation of every element of L, using disjoint split
valuations, makes the projected positive constructive. Greedily allocating
t=d/2 to ascending inert factors stays inside every valuation bound and
exhausts t because E>=t. Multiplying by the retained split representation of
the required correction uses each valuation only once. Inverting the whole
signed vector preserves target -1. Merely generating L is not this coverage.

For a campaign hit, h<p gives T<p/2 and gcd(p,T)=1. With uvk=T,
u+v=Ah and u<v, Ap=(u+v)p/h>u+v, so T<Apuk<Apvk. These denominators have
exactly two p-divisible entries and satisfy
`1/T+1/(Apuk)+1/(Apvk)=4/p`. No root-wide conclusion follows from one shift.
