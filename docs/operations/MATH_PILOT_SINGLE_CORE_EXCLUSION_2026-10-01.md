# Erdős 242: subgroup occupancy and complete single-core exclusions

Date: 2026-10-01. Local continuation; no paid API, new agent campaign,
publication, official benchmark score, or root-conjecture solution.

## Main result

For each of the two previously retained primes p=131433121 and p=159727681,
there is **no normalized divisor-led candidate at any modulus s** whose core
m=(s+1)/4 is a power of one prime. This includes arbitrarily large s, all prime
bases, all positive exponents, and the trivial m=1 endpoint. It is stronger than
checking s<=4095: a written monotone tail bound and an exact degree-one divisor
criterion make the entire single-core family decidable for each fixed p.

These are exclusions of a restricted construction, not counterexamples to
Erdős–Straus. Both primes already have strict witnesses from mixed cores:
m=36=2^2*3^2 at s=143, and m=198=2*3^2*11 at s=791 respectively.
Thus any successful candidate in this normalized family for those two p must
use at least two distinct prime factors in its core. Other representations
are not excluded. No novelty or general prime-coverage claim is established.

## 1. Separating subgroup and exponent-window obstructions

Keep the normalized family gcd(a,u)=1, au|m, s=4m-1, and s|ap+u. Its exact
modular eligibility set is

\[
R_s=\{-\prod_{\ell\mid m}\ell^{f_\ell}\pmod s:
             -v_\ell(m)\le f_\ell\le v_\ell(m)\}.
\]

Define H_s to be the subgroup of the unit group modulo s generated by all prime
factors of m. The earlier reciprocity proof gives character +1 for each
generator, hence H_s is Jacobi-positive. Consequently

\[
R_s\subseteq-H_s\subseteq\{r:(r/s)=-1\}.
\]

Every negative-character miss belongs to exactly one of two disjoint sets:
outside -H_s (a subgroup obstruction), or inside -H_s but outside R_s (an
exponent-window obstruction). The first persists even if every exponent is
allowed to grow without bound at this fixed s. The second is precisely the
additional constraint imposed by au|m. Relaxing that constraint is only a
diagnostic envelope; it is not automatically a valid fraction construction.

For prime s=q, cyclicity of the field's unit group gives the exact subgroup
membership equation (-r)^|H_q|=1 modulo q. Do not use it for composite s:
7^4=1 modulo 15, but 7 is outside the subgroup generated by 2 modulo 15.
Composite membership is computed by full generator closure.

## 2. Prime-power-core structure, including composite moduli

Let m=ell^e for a prime ell and e>=1, and put h=ord_s(ell). Coprimality forces
one of a,u to be 1. Therefore

\[
R_s=\{-\ell^f:-e\le f\le e\},\qquad
|R_s|=\min(h,2e+1).
\]

The cardinality follows because a consecutive exponent interval maps to distinct
residues until it completes one period h. No field assumption is needed.

For ell=2, h=e+2: 2^(e+2)=1 modulo s=2^(e+2)-1, while every smaller positive
power minus 1 is strictly between 0 and s. The exponent interval has length
2e+1>=e+2, so R_s=-H_s and |R_s|=e+2. The m=1 endpoint is different:
s=3, H_s={1}, R_s={2}, not the full negative coset of the subgroup generated
by 2.

For odd ell, h>2e+1. If h<=e, ell^h-1 is a positive integer smaller than s.
If e<h<=2e, write h=e+d and use ell^e=1/4 modulo s to obtain ell^d=4
modulo s; since both representatives lie below s, this would force ell^d=4.
If h=2e+1, the same relation yields ell=16 modulo s. For s>16 this forces
ell=16, impossible for an odd prime; the only smaller case, ell=3,e=1,s=11,
is checked directly. Thus |R_s|=2e+1<h: every odd single-prime core retains
an exponent-window gap even inside its full subgroup.

This is an elementary rederivation with explicit prior-art overlap, not a
claimed new theorem about Erdős–Straus.

## 3. Complete fixed-p decision: tail bound and degree-one criterion

Since ell^e=m=1/4 modulo s, the same eligibility set has positive representatives

\[
R_s=\{s-\ell^j:0\le j\le e\}
       \cup\{s-4\ell^j:0\le j<e\}.
\]

The last term 4ell^e would be s+1 and reduces to 1; it is already covered by
the f=0 term after the minus sign. Every displayed representative is at least
s-2m=(s-1)/2: ell^j<=m and 4ell^(e-1)<=2m because ell>=2.

For a fixed positive p, if s>2p+1 then p<s and its canonical residue is p,
strictly below every eligible representative. Therefore **no single-core
eligibility occurs beyond s=2p+1**, regardless of prime base or exponent.
It suffices to consider m<=(p+1)/2.

For e=1 the three eligible classes are -1,-ell,-4. Since 4ell=1 modulo s,
eligibility is equivalent to

\[
s\mid(p+1)\quad\text{or}\quad s\mid(4p+1)\quad\text{or}\quad s\mid(p+4),
\qquad s=4\ell-1,\quad \ell\text{ prime}.
\]

This enumerates every possible degree-one hit through divisors of three fixed
integers; it does not require scanning millions of prime bases. For e>=2 the
base is at most sqrt((p+1)/2). Enumerate all prime bases up to that bound, all
powers within the tail cutoff, and every normalized coprime pair. The m=1
endpoint cannot help a residual prime p=1 modulo 840, since p=1 modulo 3 but
R_3={2}. These three arguments jointly prove the finite procedure exhaustive
for this entire restricted family, not merely a bounded search window.

## 4. Dyadic ancestry and complete finite tail certificates

Write s_t=2^t-1, t>=3. If d>=3 divides t, then s_d divides s_t and reduction
maps the exact coset -<2> at s_t onto -<2> at s_d. Thus eligibility at t
implies eligibility at every such proper divisor d. For residual p=1 modulo
840, t=3 fails modulo 7, and t=4 fails modulo 15. Every composite t>=6 has
a proper divisor >=3. Hence the first eligible dyadic exponent, if it exists,
must be a prime >=5. A prime exponent does not imply a prime modulus (t=11
gives composite s=2047).

The smallest canonical dyadic eligible residue is 2^(t-1)-1. For
p=159727681, no t=2..28 qualifies, and all t>=29 are excluded because their
smallest eligible residue is at least 268435455>p. For p=131433121, all t>=28
are excluded by the corresponding bound 134217727>p. Independent factor-pair
replay confirms every finite row, including the t=2 endpoint. These narrower
all-dyadic exclusions are contained in the complete single-core result above.

prime-modulus membership. Composite unit groups need no cyclicity assumption.

Jacobi for its Type A/B trap sets. This is explicit overlap, not peer-review

For prime-power cores its two divisor branches agree with our normalized ratio
set. For mixed cores its T_m={-d,-4d:d|m} is generally a smaller set than R_s;
therefore its all-core saturation classification was NOT imported as a theorem
about our larger set. Neither author assertions of novelty nor a negative

NOT_NOVELTY_CERTIFIED; written proofs are not Lean certification or expert sign-off.
