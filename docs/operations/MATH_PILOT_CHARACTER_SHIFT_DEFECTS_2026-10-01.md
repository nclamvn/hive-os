## 1. Known construction and exact image

Let p be a prime, h a prime with h=3 mod4, p=1 mod4 and p>h. Set T=(p+h)/4. Then gcd(T,h)=1. Positive coprime u,v with uv|T and h|(u+v) give

\[
k=T/(uv),\quad A=(u+v)/h,\qquad
\frac4p=\frac1T+\frac1{Apuk}+\frac1{Apvk}.
\]

The reciprocal identity follows from u+v=Ah and 4T=p+h. With u<=v, Ap>v because p>h; thus T<Apuk. Equality u=v would force u=v=1 by coprimality, contrary to h>=3 dividing their sum. Both latter denominators and only those are p-divisible since 0<T<p. The implementation still checks strict sorted denominators and the full integer cross-product identity independently.

This is known divisor parametrization; [Tao's Proposition 1.2 and Remark 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1107.1010v2) supply the background with (a,b,c)=(u,v,h). It is not a new universal method.

Write T=product q_i^e_i and work in G=(Z/hZ)^*. The complete signed image is

\[
S=\left\{\prod_i(q_i\bmod h)^{z_i}: -e_i\le z_i\le e_i\right\}.
\]

Positive and negative net exponents go to u and v respectively; unused valuation e_i−|z_i| goes to k. All coprime u,v with uv|T arise this way, so a hit is exactly -1 in S. Canceled repeated atoms become unused mass, never an illegal common prime of u,v. k may share primes with u,v or A. A generated subgroup is not the finite signed image before saturation.

The primary retains the smallest ordered numeric-lex pair at each ratio state, without intermediate swapping. Multiplication by positive later factors preserves lex order. At the self-inverse final target -1 the smallest ordered hit automatically has u<=v. Independent replay enumerates positive coprime divisor pairs instead of this DP.

## 2. General-prime miss theorem

Let h be any prime with h=3 mod4 and let T be any positive integer coprime to h. No prime p or safe-prime restriction is required here. Let S be its complete signed image and assume **-1 is absent**. Define its exact multiplicative stabilizer

\[
H=\{r\in G:rS=S\},\qquad n=|G/H|=(h-1)/|H|,
\quad V=\sum_{q_i\bmod h\notin H}e_i.
\]

For each visible factor q_i modh outside H let d_i be its order in G/H, equivalently the smallest positive d with q_i^d modh in H. Then:

\[
n\equiv2\pmod4,\qquad 2V\le n-2,\qquad d_i>2e_i+1.
\]

If some prime-factor residue is a quadratic nonresidue, also n>=6. These are **necessary conditions for a miss**, not sufficient conditions, root impossibility, or claims about how often a shift works.

### Exact stabilizer and quotient parity

H is a subgroup. Since 1 belongs to S, if -1 belonged to H it would belong to S. Thus H excludes the unique element of order two of the cyclic group G. A subgroup of even order would contain that element, so |H| is odd. As h−1=2 times an odd integer, n is even with n=2 mod4. Also H lies in the quadratic-residue subgroup.

Let pi:G→G/H. The image pi(S) has trivial stabilizer: S is a union of H-cosets, so a period of pi(S) lifts to a period of S and is in H by exact maximality. Merely choosing the subgroup generated by factor residues would not justify this assertion.

For a visible factor, suppose d_i<=2e_i+1. Its signed interval in the quotient contains d_i consecutive powers and fills its entire cyclic subgroup. That nontrivial subgroup stabilizes the interval and therefore the whole quotient sumset after adding other factors. This contradicts trivial stabilizer. Hence d_i>2e_i+1. In particular every visible factor has order at least four; quotient order two cannot remain visible in a miss.

### Visible valuation bound

Use additive notation in the quotient. Each visible valuation unit contributes an atom {0,a,-a}. By the order condition it has three distinct elements. Factors inside H contribute only zero. Every partial sumset is aperiodic, since its period persists on adding remaining atoms and would be a period of the final quotient image.

[Kneser's addition theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.3539v1), applied in its aperiodic case, therefore gives

\[
|\pi(S)|\ge1+2V.
\]

The coset pi(-1) remains absent, so |pi(S)|<=n−1. This proves 2V<=n−2.

If some factor is QNR, it lies outside H. Were n=2, its quotient order would be two and violate the visible-order condition. Combining this with n=2 mod4 yields n>=6. This is a general-prime stabilizer reduction using known group machinery, not a novelty claim.

## 3. Character preference and the quotient-six corollary

For a shifted row, T=p/4 modh. Four is a nonzero square, so Legendre(T/h)=Legendre(p/h). A negative character means the total QNR valuation mass is odd; in particular some QNR factor exists and a pure-QR factorization is impossible. It does **not** imply -1 lies in the signed image.

A positive character means only even QNR valuation parity, not that all factors are QR. Such rows can and do hit. Consequently character is retained as a routing preference, never an impossibility rejection. The frozen experiment computes both pools and reports their minima separately.

There is a sharper conditional description if a miss has negative character and n=6. Visible QR quotient elements would have order three, forbidden by d_i>2e_i+1 for e_i>=1. Visible QNR elements can have order two or six; order two is likewise forbidden. Thus every visible factor is QNR of quotient order six. H contains only QR factors, so negative character makes V odd. The visible bound gives V<=2, hence **V=1**. The quotient image is exactly three classes {0,a,-a} and |S|=3|H|.

This is a **T-only** single-visible-unit description. It must not be conflated with an external N=pT signed box or a different five-of-six quotient result. It does not explain why the defect must disappear on another shift.
