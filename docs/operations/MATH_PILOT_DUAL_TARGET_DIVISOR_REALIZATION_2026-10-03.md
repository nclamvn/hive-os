## 1. Exact allocation, not a free modular square root

Let p be prime, p=1 modulo4; let h be prime, h=3 modulo4, h<p, and put
T=(p+h)/4. Then T<p/2 and gcd(T,h)=1. Write T=product(q_i^e_i), and let
B_T be its actual signed factor box, with each exponent in[-e_i,e_i].

The values a²d for positive ad|T are exactly the divisors of T². One
direction is immediate: alpha_i+delta_i<=e_i implies
0<=2alpha_i+delta_i<=2e_i. Conversely, every exponent j_i in[0,2e_i]
has the canonical decomposition

$$
\alpha_i=\left\lfloor\frac{j_i}{2}\right\rfloor,\quad
\delta_i=j_i\bmod2,\quad
\alpha_i+\delta_i=\left\lceil\frac{j_i}{2}\right\rceil\le e_i.
$$

Thus m|T² determines a unique squarefree-d decomposition m=a²d with
ad|T. This uniqueness concerns the normalized decomposition, not all
parametrizations or distinct Egyptian-fraction solutions. Squaring a is
algebra; it does not allocate its valuations twice.

With j_i=e_i+z_i, the norm-divisor image modulo h is T times B_T. The
Type I condition h|4a²d+1 consequently becomes

$$
\tau_I=-p^{-1}\in B_T,\qquad
\tau_{II}=-1\in B_T,\qquad p\equiv4T\pmod h.
$$

Inversion preserves membership in B_T, so -p is an equivalent Type I
membership target. It is usually a different residue. A vector at -p must
be inverted before decoding j=e+z for the chosen target -p^-1. The Type II
convention of inverting a ratio to arrange u<=v cannot silently be applied
to Type I. In norm coordinates Type I always uses -4^-1.

## 2. A target-sensitive conditional bridge

Let L be generated by the split factor residues, namely those with quadratic
character+1 modulo h. L has odd order. A generated subgroup is not an
allocated image: actual signed split exponents still have bounds[-e,e].
Their norm exponents have bounds[0,2e]. These two split images differ by
translation by the actual split part of T; either covers all L iff the other
does, but their point-correction residues differ.

Suppose there is an inert factor and the inert cosets are coherent:
q_i L is aL or a^-1 L for one anchor a. Take the smallest inert factor as
the anchor, assigning the positive direction first if both coincide. Let d
be the order of aL in G/L. Because h-1 is twice an odd integer and a is
inert, d=2 modulo4. Define directional masses E+ and E- as the sums of
actual valuations in those two directions. Norm exponents independently
fill a contiguous integer interval in the quotient:

$$
\{mL:m\mid T^2\}=a^{[-2E_-,\,2E_+]}L.
$$

Split factors contribute the identity coset. The equality follows by summing
the intervals[0,2e_i] for positive factors and[-2e_i,0] for negative ones.
This is a projection statement, not permission to use an unpaid split lift.

If 4L is outside the cyclic subgroup generated by aL, the norm target
(-4^-1)L is outside it too: (-1)L=a^(d/2)L already belongs to that subgroup.
This Type I branch misses locally. Otherwise write4L=a^beta L. The integer
beta is even by character, and

$$
\ell=\left(\frac d2-\beta\right)\bmod d
$$

is odd and nonzero, with its least positive representative in[1,d-1]. The
nearest representatives to zero of the target coset are ell and ell-d.
Therefore its projected membership is **exactly**

$$
2E_+\ge\ell\quad\text{or}\quad2E_-\ge d-\ell.
$$

Failure is a local miss independent of split coverage. Success is not yet
a full hit. Choose an available representative, allocate inert norm powers
within their actual bounds, and obtain their product w. The correction
(-4^-1)/w must occur in the actual split **norm** image. If it does, retain
the realizing split vector. If it does not and coverage is incomplete,
the greedy attempt is UNKNOWN: another allocation may work. Actual complete
split coverage makes projected feasibility sufficient.

For Type II, the analogous signed inert interval is[-E,E], E=E++E-;
(-1)L occurs iff E>=d/2. A ratio correction from the actual signed split
image is still required. Missing a selected correction is not a no-solution
theorem.

### The sharper threshold when 4 belongs to L

In this case beta=0 and ell=d/2, so Type I projection is exactly

$$
\max(E_+,E_-)\ge\left\lceil\frac d4\right\rceil.
$$

This is a directional maximum, not merely total mass. At d6, masses(1,1)
have total2 but miss the Type I projection; masses(2,0) hit it. Type II in
both cases still needs total3. These are algebraic controls, not prime-root
claims. If p=1 modulo8 and2|T, reciprocity gives2 split, hence4 belongs to
L; the implication is not valid for every p=1 modulo4. More generally the
tool computes beta, rather than assuming it vanishes.

## 3. Realizing the actual Type I certificate

Once the full norm vector j satisfies4m=-1 modulo h, decode its squarefree
decomposition m=a²d and let

$$
c=\frac{T}{ad},\quad v=\frac{4a^2d+1}{h},\quad
b=cv-a=\frac{pa+c}{h}.
$$

All are integers; hb=pa+c follows from4acd=p+h. Since p>h, b>a>0;
since c<=T<p/2<ap, the denominators are strictly ordered:

$$
\frac4p=\frac1T+\frac1{bcd}+\frac1{abdp},\qquad T<bcd<abdp.
$$

The cleared identity follows from4abcd=b(p+h)=p(a+b)+c. None of a,c,d is
p-divisible as each is at most T<p; hb=pa+c also makes b nonzero modulo p.
Exactly one denominator is p-divisible. The tool records the allocated
exponents, m,a,d,c,v,b and actual correction, not just a model's claim that
a modular square root exists.

## 6. What remains a research problem

The upgraded construction spends actual arithmetic support and has a precise
failure diagnosis. It does not supply a uniformly eligible h<p with enough
directional mass and paid split correction for every unresolved p. The next
candidate lemma must establish that root-coupled supply, or a target-specific
paid point lift, for a stated structural family; it must survive a separate
counterexample search and primary-literature comparison before admission.

No gain on exposed controls is called a discovery. Six gates remain open:
universal root existence, novelty, formal/external review, governed tool/UI/
agent admission, uncontaminated equal-budget live-model comparison, and
product E2E. Same-model/host AI contexts are useful independent implementations,
not an external mathematical council. Billing, API spending, deployment and
Git publication are outside this slice.
