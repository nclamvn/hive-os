# Erdős242: an infinite power barrier and a divisor-realization pivot

2026-10-03. TIP044, research-only. **Original problem NOT_ESTABLISHED.**
This note proves a limitation of one proposed mechanism, then restores a

## 1. Why change direction

TIP043 obtained actual, independently replayed certificates but did not bridge
eventual supply of a small quadratic nonresidue to sufficient *allocated*
valuation and a target-sensitive correction. Another enumeration on the same
180 exposed rows would not prove that bridge. Here the bridge is challenged
algebraically before further search.

Throughout, p is prime, p congruent to 1 modulo 4; h is prime, h congruent to
3 modulo 4, and h<p. Set

$$
T=\frac{p+h}{4}<\frac p2.
$$

The restricted Type II construction searches coprime positive u,v with uv|T
and h|(u+v). Its factor ratios use signed exponents whose absolute values do
not exceed their actually available valuations. This is one construction at
one shift, not the set of all Egyptian-fraction representations.

## 2. An infinite obstruction to the single-11 power mechanism

**Proposition.** For every prime p congruent to 2521 modulo 9240, its least
positive quadratic nonresidue is 11. If 11|T, a signed box using only 11 with
available budget 1<=e<=4 cannot reach -1 modulo h.

Proof. The progression imposes p=1 modulo 840 and p=2 modulo 11. The primes
2,3,5,7 are quadratic residues modulo p by the supplementary laws and
reciprocity. Every integer from 1 to 10 factors into these primes. Meanwhile
(11/p)=(p/11)=(2/11)=-1, since p=1 modulo 4. Thus the least nonresidue is 11.

From 11|T follows h=9 modulo 11. Because h=3 modulo 4,
(11/h)=-(h/11)=-1. Write D=ord_h(11). Since h-1 is twice an odd integer and
11 is a nonresidue, D is even and D=2 modulo 4. The cyclic subgroup has
11^(D/2)=-1, and the smallest absolute exponent representing -1 is D/2.
Consequently a hit with e<=4 forces D<=8, hence D=2 or D=6.

For D=2, h divides 11+1=12. For D=6, h divides
11^3+1=1332=2^2*3^2*37. Among those prime divisors, only h=3 is 3 modulo 4;
but it is not 9 modulo 11. Contradiction.

The residue and modulus are coprime, so there are infinitely many such primes
by [Dirichlet's theorem, Theorem18.1](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2019fa/LectureNotes18.pdf).
The finite replay is not what proves infinitude.

**Quantifiers matter.** Available e means actual valuation or deliberately
allocated copies. Forcing 11^4|T does not force v_11(T)=4: accidental higher
valuation is outside this proposition. Other factors, other shifts, paid split
corrections, and Type I remain allowed. For example p=2521,h=31,T=638 has a
full Type II witness using u=2,v=29,k=11. The 11 factor is unused.

### A sharper canonical-supply counterfamily

For primes p=41161 modulo 101640, equivalently p=1 modulo 840 and p=21
modulo 484, the least nonresidue is again 11 (now p=10 modulo 11). The least
prime in h=-p modulo 44 is exactly 23. Here T=11 modulo 121, hence
v_11(T)=1. The signed single-atom image is {1,11,21} modulo 23, missing 22.
Again gcd(41161,101640)=1 makes the prime family infinite. This refutes a
specific *least progression prime plus least nonresidue* shortcut, not the
full factor support at that shift.

## 3. What existing analytic supply does and does not guarantee

If q<=C p^alpha and the reduced progression h=-p modulo 4q^e is used to
force q^e|T, a Linnik exponent lambda yields

$$
h\le C_e p^{\alpha\lambda e}.
$$

This guarantees h<p eventually when alpha*lambda*e<1. The selected classical
inputs in TIP043, alpha=.16 and lambda=5.2, give .832e: e=1 works, e=2 does
not give that guarantee. This is failure of an upper-bound argument, not
impossibility of a small h. For fixed q and e, there are only finitely many
reduced residue classes, so their least primes have a fixed finite maximum;
they eventually lie below p. The issue is a *uniform moving-q* conclusion.

Sources checked at their statements: [Banks–Guo v1, Introduction(1.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.05523v1)
for the Burgess exponent, and [Xylouris, Acta Arithmetica150(2011), Theorem1.1](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83078)
for 5.18, weakened here to 5.2. No numerical cutoff, GRH guarantee or new
bound is extracted.

### A necessary root-size barrier in the coherent quotient

Root reciprocity from TIP043 identifies each inert prime factor of T as a
quadratic nonresidue modulo p, so it is at least the root's least nonresidue
q0. Suppose the inert factor cosets in G/L are coherent, in {aL,a^-1L},
where L is generated by split factors, and let d=ord(aL), E the total inert
valuation. Their projected signed box reaches (-1)L only if E>=d/2. Therefore

$$
q_0^{d/2}\le q_0^E\le\prod_{q\ \mathrm{inert}}q^{v_q(T)}\le T<\frac p2
$$

is necessary for a hit in this coherent construction. It is not sufficient:
the correcting element of L must still be realized with actual split support.
Here d is the **quotient** order, not the full order of q0. For p=401,h=31,
T=108, ord_31(3)=30 but the actual split factor 2^2 covers a subgroup of
order5; the quotient order is6. A budget of three 3's suffices with the paid
correction 2^-2. Pruning using 30/2 would incorrectly remove a genuine hit.
Incoherent profiles are outside this size argument.

## 4. The complementary Type I norm construction

The following is a specialization of a known parametrization, not an invented
method: [Elsholtz–Tao v6, Proposition2.3 and(2.6)–(2.8)](https://arxiv.org/html/1107.1010v6).
On the **same T**, seek positive integers a,d such that

$$
ad\mid T,\qquad h\mid 4a^2d+1.
$$

Define

$$
c=\frac{T}{ad},\qquad v=\frac{4a^2d+1}{h},\qquad
b=cv-a=\frac{pa+c}{h}.
$$

These are integers: hc v=c(4a^2d+1)=4aT+c=a(p+h)+c, giving the last
equality. It also shows b>a since p>h and c>0. Hence x=acd=T is smaller
than y=bcd. Since c<=T<p/2 and a>=1, ap>c, so y<z=abdp. All three are
positive and strictly ordered. Multiplication by abcdp proves the identity:

$$
\frac4p=\frac1{acd}+\frac1{bcd}+\frac1{abdp},\qquad
4abcd=p(a+b)+c.
$$

Indeed 4abcd=b(p+h), and hb=pa+c. Exactly one denominator is divisible
by p. None of a,c,d is divisible by p because each is at most T<p; modulo
p the equation hb=pa+c forces b nonzero, as h and c are nonzero.

If T has prime factorization product(q_i^e_i), the legal allocations for a,d
obey alpha_i+delta_i<=e_i. The exponent 2alpha_i+delta_i in a^2d is algebra,
not permission to spend twice the support of a. The complementary c retains
the unused factors. If d is inert modulo h, -1/(4d) is a square modulo h;
nevertheless its square root must be realized by an **actual divisor**
a|T/d. Existence of a residue root alone does not provide that divisor.

### Exact prime-T comparison

If T=r is prime and h>3, the possible (a,d) are (1,1),(r,1),(1,r).
The first would require h|5 and is excluded; the second would require -1
to be a square modulo h and is excluded. Thus this Type I branch hits iff
4r=-1 modulo h, with (a,d,c)=(1,r,1).

The restricted coprime-divisor Type II box has only (1,1) and (1,r), up to
swap. It hits iff r=-1 modulo h. The two conditions are disjoint for h>3:
together they would imply h|3. Both require an inert r, but other inert
residues need not satisfy either condition. They do not cover all primes.

## 5. Source screen and the next actual research question

[Chamberland2026, Integers26 A42](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/aa42/aa42.pdf)
provides another Type II equivalence; its all-prime existence remains a
conjecture, not an available universal solution. [Pollack v1, Theorem1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1508.05035v1)
supplies many small prime character nonresidues, not divisors of this moving
T nor a bounded target correction. This source screen is not exhaustive
prior-art or novelty certification.

The justified next attack is **divisor realization**, not another claim that
nonresidue supply alone guarantees a hit: can a root-bound choice of h<p
force actual a,d with ad|T and h|4a^2d+1, or an actual Type II target
correction, for a specified unresolved structural family? Any proposed lemma
must state its modulus, uniform bounds and support allocation, survive an
independent counterexample search, and be checked against these classical
parametrizations. No such universal lemma has been proved in TIP044.
