## 1. Splitting is coupled to the root, not independently random per shift

Let p be a prime congruent to1 modulo4, h<p a prime congruent to3 modulo4,
and T=(p+h)/4. Then gcd(T,h)=1. For every odd prime q dividing T,

$$
\left(\frac qh\right)=\left(\frac pq\right).
$$

Indeed h=-p moduloq, and quadratic reciprocity gives
(q/h)=(-1/q)(h/q)=(-1/q)(-p/q)=(p/q). If2 dividesT then h=-p modulo8,
so the supplementary quadratic characters of2 at h and p also agree.
For p=1 modulo840,2,3,5,7 all have character+1. Thus every inert shifted
factor is larger than7, and the character of any prime factor is determined
by p even when h changes. This is an elementary consequence of
[quadratic reciprocity](https://math.mit.edu/~dav/cheatSheetCORR2.pdf), not
a new reciprocity theorem.

## 2. A general asymptotic supply lemma from classical bounds

For every sufficiently large residual prime p=1 modulo840 there exists a
prime h=3 modulo4 and a prime q>7 such that

$$
h\le C p^{0.832}<p,\qquad q\mid\frac{p+h}{4},\qquad
\left(\frac qh\right)=-1.
$$

Here C is independent of p. No numerical C or starting threshold is derived.

Proof. Let q be the least positive quadratic nonresidue modulop. It is prime:
if composite, all its smaller prime factors have character+1, contradicting
multiplicativity. Also q<p. Put epsilon=0.16−1/(4sqrt(e))>0. The classical
Burgess bound q<=C_epsilon p^0.16 is stated in Introduction equation1.1 of
[Banks–Guo, version1](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.05523v1). Apply the published
[Xylouris Theorem1.1, p66](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83078)
to the reduced progression a=-p modulo M=4q. It gives the least prime
h<=C_L M^5.18, hence also the weaker h<=C_L M^5.2. Consequently

$$
h\le C_L(4C_\epsilon)^{5.2}p^{0.16\cdot5.2}
=Cp^{0.832}.
$$

Since0.832<1, h<p eventually. The progression gives h=3 modulo4 and
q|T. Since p=1 modulo4, reciprocity gives (p/q)=(q/p)=-1;
Section1 then gives (q/h)=-1. Residualp excludesq=2,3,5,7.
This proves the stated corollary. The published5.18 exponent could sharpen
the arithmetic exponent to0.8288, but the frozen proposal uses0.832.

This is a **derived classical corollary**, not a claim of original research.
It rules out persistent absence of all inert shifted factors when the shift
may grow with p. It does NOT show (p/h)=-1: other inert valuations can make
the total character positive. It supplies neither sufficient valuation mass,
favorable multiplicative order, paid split coverage nor a TypeII witness.
It gives no certificate for the finite roots in this repository.

## 3. Exact inverse-direction obstruction, with paid support

Factor T=product q_i^e_i. A legal signed vector has −e_i<=z_i<=e_i and
residue product q_i^z_i in G=U(h). Its integer pair is
u=product q_i^max(z_i,0), v=product q_i^max(-z_i,0), so gcd(u,v)=1
and uv|T. A legal target−1 is equivalent to h|(u+v) for this shift.

Let L be generated by the split-factor residues, those with character+1.
It lies in the quadratic-residue subgroup and has odd order. **Generation
does not pay for coverage:** the bounded split signed box may be smaller.
If no inert factor exists, the full legal image is contained in L and misses−1.

Otherwise choose the smallest inert prime's coset aL. Suppose every inert
factor coset is aL or a^-1L. Let d=ord(aL) and E=sum inert e_i. The quotient
is cyclic with twice odd order, so d is even and congruent to2 modulo4.
The unique involution is (−1)L=a^(d/2)L. With inverse directions epsilon_i
in{+1,−1}, every legal inert vector projects to a^tL where
t=sum epsilon_i z_i. Sums of the independent integer intervals fill exactly
[−E,E]. Therefore

$$
\pi(S_{\mathrm{inert}})=\{a^tL:-E\le t\le E\},\qquad
(-1)L\in\pi(S_{\mathrm{inert}})\iff E\ge d/2.
$$

The least absolute representative of d/2 modulod has size d/2, proving both
directions. In particular E<d/2 proves an exact **local shift miss**, without
enumerating the whole signed target image. d may be smaller than [G:L].

For E>=d/2, allocate t=d/2 across the ascending inert support within each
valuation budget. Its actual product w has correction−1/w in L. The registered
sufficient rule requires full split-box coverage before constructing that
correction; full coverage guarantees a legal representation. It is sufficient,
not necessary: a particular correction might exist even in an unpaid box,
but this positive branch remains UNDETERMINED there. The supports are disjoint, so combining
them pays every valuation once and yields the actual target. Thus, under
both coherence AND full paid split coverage, hit iff E>=d/2.
Without that coverage, reaching the target coset is insufficient. Incoherent
directions also receive no hit/miss verdict from this lemma.

For a real row put k=T/(uv), A=(u+v)/h. The legal pair yields

$$
\frac4p=\frac1T+\frac1{Apuk}+\frac1{Apvk}.
$$

This uses the classical coprime-divisor TypeII construction, not a new
parametrization; see [Tao, selected historicalv2, Proposition1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1107.1010v2).
The diagnostics additionally check exact integer identity, strict ordered
denominators, and exactly two entries divisible by p. A local miss never
excludes other shifts, composite shifts, TypeI or all TypeII representations.

## Remaining root obligation

The supply corollary establishes one inert divisor for an eventually available
shift. A root proof still needs enough bounded multiplicative coverage:
coherent directions with E>=d/2 AND paid split correction, or a different
proved target-sensitive expansion mechanism for incoherent directions.
**Direction diversity alone is not sufficient.** Neither a least nonresidue,
negative total character, generated span nor a fixed enlarged menu closes this
gap. Formal/external review, exhaustive novelty clearance, governed agent/UI
