## 1. Sufficient theorem

Let G be a finite abelian group, with distinguished target t. Let K be a
subgroup already covered by legal representations using spent support J.
For disjoint unused support B, let factor i have unit residue r_i and valuation
e_i, and let D be the subgroup generated by r_i K in G/K. Its legal signed
box is

$$
S=\left\{\prod_{i\in B}(r_iK)^{z_i}: -e_i\le z_i\le e_i\right\}\subseteq D.
$$

For every proper subgroup H of D, put n=|D/H| and d_i=ord(r_i H). Assume

$$
1+\sum_{i\in B}\bigl(\min(d_i,2e_i+1)-1\bigr)\ge n.
$$

Then S=D. Indeed, if S were proper, its exact stabilizer H would be proper.
The image of S in D/H is aperiodic. Every prefix is also aperiodic: its
periods persist after multiplying further atoms. A projected consecutive
signed interval has exactly min(d_i,2e_i+1) elements. Iterated Kneser gives
the displayed lower bound for |S/H|, contradicting a proper image.

The underlying result is [DeVos, Theorem 1 (Kneser)](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.3539v1).
The all-subgroup sufficient deduction above is independently derived here;
it is not claimed new. Neither cyclic D nor individually primitive generators
are required. Trivial H already forces |D|<=2W+1, W=sum(e_i).
The theorem is sufficient, not necessary: an image can fill D while failing
this particular criterion or its small-group bound. Coverage of D does not
imply target membership in D.

## 2. Paid construction and frozen policy

The covered subgroup K is not a free exact stabilizer of a full factor image.
Its representatives have actually spent distinct valuations. For each unused
factor, the established ascending individual policy first tests relative
order d<=2e+1 and uses exactly d consecutive digits beginning at -e. Factors
skipped earlier are reconsidered after any enlargement, including order 1.

Only when no individual is eligible are multi-factor blocks tested, in
ascending cardinality then ascending support bitmask. Each attempt binds
current K, current unused support, exact ascending atom-order prefix, W and
bound. Positive-generator quotient closure uses numeric-min frontier and
ascending generators. If it exceeds the bound, the retained prefix proves
only a lower bound; it is not an exact D or a target absence certificate.

For a fully closed D, TypeScript extends subgroups by generator closure and
retains the complete proper lattice, canonically sorted by size then numeric
member list. Even failed inequalities remain visible. Python independently
reconstructs the lattice by joins of cyclic subgroups. A resource-limited
lattice is PARTIAL, never a completed rejection.

Only theorem PASS permits block representative extraction. TypeScript uses
signed-coordinate DP; Python uses positive coprime divisor pairs. Both retain
the numeric-lex **ordered** pair per quotient coset, actual residue and vector.
Multiplying each block representative by each previous K representative once
uses disjoint support and covers the preimage subgroup. The predicted complete
D is checked before consumption. First eligible block is consumed, individual
selection restarts, and the first covered target stops the chain.

When no block passes, remaining disjoint support uses the unchanged energy
criterion: L=ceil(N²/E), E=sum(mu²), with strict LA+LB>|G/K|. Equality is not
enough for energy intersection, unlike >= in the block theorem. A separately
paid K lift and whole-vector inversion preserve valuations. Root-bound

tuple (T,Apuk,Apvk), including its exact Egyptian-fraction identity and exactly
two p-divisible denominators. Witnesses are constructive, not globally minimal.
