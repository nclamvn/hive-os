## Symbolic shift argument and exact debt

Assume LRC(m) for all 1<=m<=12 in the moving-speed convention. Let u=(u_1,...,u_14) be positive integers, d prime, E={i:d does not divide u_i}, e=|E|>=2 and m_d=ceil(2d/15), with e*m_d<d. There are 14-e<=12 divisible coordinates. The strict inequality also excludes e=14, so the divisible subproblem is nonempty.

For i outside E write v_i=u_i/d. The lower theorem supplies a real tau satisfying ||tau*v_i||>=1/(15-e). Put t_0=tau/d. For j=0,...,d-1,

`(t_0+j/d)*u_i = tau*v_i+j*v_i`.

Thus every divisible coordinate keeps its distance, and 1/(15-e)>=1/15. For i in E, u_i mod d is a nonzero unit, so j -> u_i*j mod d permutes the d equally spaced residues. For an arbitrary real phase alpha, the strict bad arc ||alpha+a*j/d||<1/15 has length 2/15 on the circle. Its intersection with a d-grid contains at most ceil(2d/15) points: after lifting an open arc to the real line, k grid points span (k-1)/d<2/15; the strict endpoint condition gives the stated integer bound. Wrapping changes the choice of lift, not the count. Equality is GOOD.

The union of all e exception bad sets contains at most e*m_d<d shifts. Choose j outside it; t=t_0+j/d works for all fourteen speeds. This is the known conditional shift lemma, not a new theorem or global LRC reproduction.

The prose is a symbolic conditional argument requiring independent source/statement alignment review. The finite consumer cannot manufacture tau, prove the lower theorems, or certify all real phases merely by evaluating representatives. Complete critical boundaries and connected open-cell constancy are retained as an explicitly reviewed analytic alignment premise. No formal kernel is integrated.
